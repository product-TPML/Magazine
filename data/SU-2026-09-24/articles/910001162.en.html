Dr. Sugami Ramesh

‘Look how smart you are? Learn from the child next door. How silent, how obedient. Children should be like that...’ Parents often advise their children like this, comparing them with other children. But often, we don't realize that the excessive obedience and silence of the child next door can also be a psychological problem.

In English, we call such a child an 'easy child'. This child, who never gets angry, rarely seeks attention, easily adapts to changing schedules, and completes their work without anyone's motivation, grows up without parental interference. They don't need the emotional energy of their parents. Such children are more common in families with financial insecurity, severe parental illness, mental instability, drug abuse, or parental marital problems. This child is seen as the family's protector, a miracle. This calm nature of the child is rewarded with much praise. Such children are labeled 'good', 'family support', or 'never have to worry about the child for a moment' and are shown off to the world.

In reality, a person should act according to their age. That is a sign of normal development. Children at their age should have stubbornness, anger, and playfulness. These are all psychological motivations. Only when these are shown does it become clear that the child is developing normally. Otherwise, there should be concern that there might be a problem.

May be dangerous

Underneath this calm, perfect nature of children, a deep and dangerous psychological state develops that threatens their long-term development. A child should naturally have intense emotional fluctuations, confusing desires, demands, or defiant behavior. But in such children, it does not develop. These are fundamental, undeniable biological realities of human psychological development. If children completely lack these expressions, it does not mean they will develop uniquely; it means these children have made a calculated, terrifying, and completely unconscious decision to suppress these traits.

The child observes the stress, weakness, or violence around their home and creates an equation within themselves: 'My parents are not normal. If I show a single need, if I cry, or if I constantly seek their attention, I risk losing them' - this feeling develops in the child's mind. They conclude that they will abandon me, the family will split, so my survival and demise are entirely in my nature.

Such a realization builds a beautiful, golden cage within the child's mind. The praise the child receives for this calm nature acts as a psychological agent reinforcing the suppression of their emotions. It holds back their own human needs for love, security, and being together with everyone in the family. The child learns to consider their own natural impulses, desires, anger, and signs of suffering as dangerous things.

Impact in adulthood

When such an 'easy child' reaches adulthood, the danger of their buried emotions begins to appear. The crucial psychological muscles needed to identify, assert, and protect their own personal boundaries have not developed in them. These individuals are susceptible to situations like severe exploitation, professional failure, and toxic relationships.

Even if saying 'yes' causes severe harm to their health, finances, or personal matters, they cannot bravely say 'no'. This is because the deeply rooted neurological vulnerability from childhood does not allow them to say no. It does not allow them to share traumatic experiences. They hide serious medical problems, severe financial crises, or any internal problems.

This massive amount of psychological suppression in them often manifests through severe, chronic somatization, a problem of psychological stress. The psychological stress, anger, and sadness denied vocal expression try to find an alternative path through the body's physical tissue. This usually emerges as problems like gastrointestinal disorders, unresolved migraines, chronic pain, and tension.

Humans cannot hold onto emotions for long. Whether it's crying or laughing. They are a kind of psychological stress. We feel relieved by sharing them with our closest ones. Similarly, children also express what they want in their own way. These can be controlled to some extent, but they cannot be completely curbed. Do not take excessive obedience lightly. If children don't talk, if they prefer to be alone, try to get them to mingle with you. Make sure they are social. That will make their youth bright.
