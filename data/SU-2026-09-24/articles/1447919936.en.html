D.G. Mallikarjuna

A small Moroccan village nestled on a hillside - its name is Chefchaouen. The story here is different! Here, the walls of the houses, the doors, the stairs - everything is one color, blue! It feels as if someone has brought the sky down and poured it over this town. This is not an exaggeration, but a truth seen with my own eyes!

I love the color blue. It's a color that brings peace to the mind and coolness to the eyes. So, the moment I stepped into this town, I was literally lost. There are so many confusing alleys here that even Google Maps would throw up its hands and say, 'Everything looks the same here, you find your way!'
Walking through the winding, sloping lanes, I didn't feel like taking another step forward. Every turn was a new photoshoot spot! Standing by one such blue door, those who came with us held Rumi's book in their hands and had me take their picture. Blue wall, Rumi in hand, a peaceful smile on the face... - that frame was no less than a postcard! What one of our group members said then felt so true - 'The phone battery will run out here, but the enthusiasm to take photos will not!'

What is the story behind this blue color?

Now this question must have come to your mind too - why is the whole town painted blue? Weren't there other colors in the shop?!
Once upon a time, Jewish refugees settled here. To keep the memory of their old homeland, the sacred color of the sky and the sea alive, they painted their houses blue. If the color faded or was bleached by the sun, they would paint it again! Is there such a devoted tradition anywhere else? If we don't paint our house walls for a year, we get called lazy, but the people here follow this like a ritual!

Even though the Jewish population is not large today, the villagers have meticulously preserved this blue heritage they left behind. In a way, the whole town is a living museum!

I must tell you an interesting fact here - this blue color doesn't have just one story, but three or four stories! According to one story, during the Spanish Inquisition (around 1492), the fleeing Jews transferred the tradition of blue threads in their prayer shawls to the walls. Another story says it was a contribution of the Jews who came in the 1930s, escaping Hitler's persecution during World War II. Another interesting (and somewhat practical!) argument is that it was to repel mosquitoes. The theory is that mosquitoes are fooled by the blue color, thinking it's water! If you ask the local guides which story is true, each one gives a different answer. - So, how much is true, and how much is a story made up for tourists, we don't know. - But the walls are blue, that much is certain!

Another surprising fact - this town was founded in 1471 by a chieftain named Moulay Ali Ben Rachid. It was just a small fort built to resist Portuguese attacks. The name 'Chefchaouen' comes from the Berber word 'Ichawen' - meaning 'goat horns'! This is because the two mountain peaks surrounding the town look exactly like goat horns from a distance. Another interesting fact - this town closed its doors to Christians for about 450 years! History says that until Spain conquered it in 1920, only a handful of Europeans had stepped into this town. So, a secret blue world that was kept hidden for so many years is now being visited by people from all over the world. This must be the play of time!

Cinema, Literature, this Blue City...

Morocco itself is a favorite destination for Hollywood - a series of films and series like 'Lawrence of Arabia', 'Gladiator', 'Game of Thrones' have been filmed in its barren lands and forts. But what's strange is that - such a photogenic Chefchaouen has not yet become the hero of any major film! This shy actress, who is not easily captured by cameras, has only become famous on Instagram and in travel documentaries so far. It's like a singer who became famous in the studio without ever going to a concert!

Coming to literature, the city of Tangier, two hours away from here, was the second home of American writer Paul Bowles. His novel 'The Sheltering Sky', later made into a film by Bertolucci, introduced the magic of Morocco's deserts and mountains to the entire Western world. This entire region is the land of Sufi saints, nomadic poets, and visionaries. Blue walls, narrow lanes, silence - all these create a meditative atmosphere!

The Kingdom of Cats!

I must tell you an amusing fact here. Throughout Morocco, especially in this town, cats are highly respected! In front of every door, on every step, a cat will be comfortably sleeping, with the air of owning the entire town. Perhaps because of the tradition that Prophet Muhammad loved cats, the people here do not chase these cats away, but instead feed and care for them. It's as if a photo is incomplete without a blue door and a cat next to it - they have become the 'local models' here!

Sightseeing and Feasting

I didn't even realize how three hours passed while wandering around like this. It seems like time doesn't exist in Chefchaouen. Perhaps the clocks here are also painted blue and taking a rest!

That day, a traditional Moroccan meal awaited us. As soon as we sat down in a small, home-like restaurant, a hot, pure tomato soup - the first sip brought comfort. Then came the main star of the day, 'Tajine'. A dish cooked in a special pot with a conical clay lid. The smoke that comes out when the lid is removed, that aroma, oh my! Juicy potatoes, carrots, beans, a little rice on top... the taste was truly amazing.

The almond ice cream that came next was also superb. Even though I was full, I couldn't resist the desire to eat one more spoonful!
This hilly region is famous for goat farming. So, goat cheese and goat meat dishes are common on the menus here. Vegetarians also have plenty of options. Vegetable couscous, various types of bread, olives, herbal tea, and Moroccan mint tea will remain a sweet memory for everyone who visits here.

In short, Chefchaouen is not just a tourist destination, it's an experience. When you leave here, your camera's memory card will be full, and your heart will be full too. But one strange desire will remain; to paint our house walls blue too!g
