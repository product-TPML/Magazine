If you hit an anthill

G.V. Anandamurthy

In the currently available 'Manusmriti', it is stated that 'women are not worthy of freedom'. We are hearing and seeing this ambivalent stance again and again in all fields in India.
This statement is available in 'Manusmriti', which is said to have been composed in the 2nd century BC. Manu is said to be one of the most ancient sages of India. Manusmriti, which created patriarchy, also states that 'whatever a wife, son and slave earn becomes the property of the master'. In addition to this, Shudras and women, who are fellow human beings belonging to the Dalit community, are denied the right to freedom, education, property and equality. 'Woman is the source of insult, woman is the source of conflict. Woman is the source of worldly existence. Therefore, abandon woman', Manusmriti attacked the self-respect of women. Through this, the perception that men are superior and women are inferior spread everywhere, and the opportunities available to men in our social and cultural spheres are abundant. This has fundamentally caused the vitality and creativity of women to wither. Through this, a sense of inferiority has been created in women without reason. Because of such irrationalities, no single female philosopher has been created in the mainstream tradition in India, except for some mystical traditions!

There are also modern people among us who believe in Sanatana Dharma without doubt, saying 'everything Manu said is medicine'. These statements that suppress the vitality, self-respect and freedom of women are not acceptable, no matter what religion on earth said them. That is why Babasaheb Ambedkar burnt 'Manusmriti' in Mahad, Maharashtra in 1927, saying 'customs and traditions are enemies of novelty'.

Recently, in a convention of female students in Pune, an Adivasi girl publicly made statements that expose the cruelty created by patriarchy. Adivasi female students living in the hostel, if they go home from the hostel and return after a month, they must bring a medical certificate stating that they are not pregnant and give it to the head of the hostel. Otherwise, they will not be allowed to enter the hostel! This is the height of cruel behavior towards women. Even though they know that such cruelty is terrible, the female students are silently enduring the insults and suffering inside, unable to condemn it. Because their family circumstances, social and economic background all silence them. Such incidents that destroy the self-confidence of women should not happen in any corner of the world! This statement is as much related to women as it is to humanity. Only a humanism that understands that women also have a thinking and contemplating mind, and that includes everyone, can eradicate such discriminatory feelings deeply rooted in society.

Socialist thinker Kishan Patnaik, who strongly opposed the bondage that male society has imposed on women socially and culturally, said, 'Don't despair! Even when your dignity is attacked, maintain your self-respect. All religious organizations and institutions that claim to liberate women are hypocritical and will achieve nothing; but they will only double the state of inferiority and quadruple social and personal perversion. The protective weapon that a woman should have is not nails and teeth; nor is it a pistol – but what she should have for herself is a very sharp sense of self-respect. Everything else will become clear to her on its own. A woman must fight for her rights'. Gandhiji, who said that if there is no care for women, the human race will be destroyed, said, 'If I had been born a woman, I would have rebelled against the prestige of men who say that women are born to be toys for men. I have become a woman in my mind to go into her heart'. To understand the insecurity and insults that women experience every moment, not only Gandhiji, but the entire society needs to become women in their minds. What needs to happen now is a transformation into a healthy, pure and mutually understanding state between men and women. In the famous writer Franz Kafka's novel 'Metamorphosis', when its protagonist wakes up in the morning, he finds himself transformed into an insect. Similarly, what if all the men in the world were transformed into women one beautiful morning!

The words of illiterate folk women who have suffered immense hardships created by male society melt even stony hearts: 'Only the mother who gave birth knows the sorrow of daughters / The heat of the snake inside the anthill / What does Shiva on the head know!', 'It is better to be born as earth than to be born as a woman / If born as a tree on the earth / It would provide shade to the fortunate'. We know various kinds of slavery in the world. But the inferior slavery imposed on women is a disgrace to humanity.
