Embroidery art holds a unique place in the Banjara community, which is famous for its colorful and vibrant attire. Embroidery designs are paramount not only in everyday wear but also in festivals and auspicious occasions. Among these, red, pink, and blue are considered colors that represent the Lambani community. The beauty of the embroidery, done with threads in intricate designs on fabrics of these and other colors, is not only visually appealing but also creates a distinct cultural identity for the community compared to others.

Not just colored threads, but Banjara garments made using coins, cowrie shells, shiny mirrors, and white metal instantly attract and enchant viewers. The varied designs of Banjara embroidery captivate not only women but also men. Banjara embroidery art is so attractive that it appeals not only to locals but also to foreigners.

Among Banjara embroidery art, the embroidery art of the Sandur region of Karnataka is unique in the country. The fact that this art, which blossoms in the delicate fingers of Lambani women in this region, received geographical indication (GI Tag-134) in 2010, is an honor for the art of that community.

Although Sandur immediately brings to mind its mining and politics, in recent decades, Sandur Lambani embroidery art has also flourished. Behind this is the care and commitment of the Ghorpade family, the famous royal family of the Sandur region. The Sandur Kushala Kala Kendra, formed as a result of this family's interest, has been working for three decades to preserve Lambani embroidery art and empower Lambani women. More than 500 Lambani community artisans in Ballari and Vijayanagara districts have built their livelihoods through khadi fabric production and embroidery art.

Specialty of Sandur Lambani Art

There is a vast difference between embroidery art in other geographical areas and Lambani embroidery art in the Sandur region. The colors of the fabrics used in Lambani embroidery in this region, the different embroidery designs, and the use of natural materials have given Sandur embroidery art its uniqueness.

Mainly, assembling small pieces of colored fabric by hand to prepare the entire garment, and especially the 'patchwork' made from small pieces of unwanted fabric, gives an artistic beauty to the attire. It is special that there are no human or animal forms in this embroidery design. Sandur Lambani embroidered clothes, adorned with mirrors, beads, buttons-cowrie shells, coins, small bells, wool tassels, and other metal ornaments, captivate the viewers. These embroidered clothes enhance the beauty of the wearer. Wall hangings, sofa cushion covers, and table runners hung on the walls of the house give a cultural touch and a rich look to the home.

The use of natural designs such as trees, vines, flowers, leaves in embroidery, and traditional hand block prints like 'Thoori' and 'Vakli' designed only by the Lambani community of the Ballari region, along with 14 other types of embroidery designs including Jholya, Bakya, Maki, Kilan, Thera Doora, Koudi, Relo, Gadhi, Nakra, are the reasons why Sandur Lambani embroidery art received geographical indication (GI Tag), says Suryaprabha A. Ghorpade, President of Sandur Kushala Kala Kendra.

Various types of experiments with Lambani embroidery have also been done on kora, silk, and khadi fabrics. Many products including sarees, kurtas, handbags, purses, cushion covers, wall hangings, table runners, necklaces, and rakhis are manufactured at this center.

Guinness Record at G20

In 2023, at the G20 summit held in Hampi, the Sandur Kushala Kala Kendra, in collaboration with the Ministry of Culture, created a Guinness World Record for the largest exhibition of Lambani art. The largest textile exhibition of 1,755 Lambani embroidery artworks made by about 450 women of the art center was also praised by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

At this same Kushala Kala Kendra, senior artist Shanti Bai has become a conservator of a rare artistic tradition by training hundreds of Lambani women in Lambani embroidery. In 2001, a wall hanging prepared by Shantibai created a new sensation in the world of handicrafts. In it, she depicted festivals, dances, and daily life of the Lambani culture through delicate stitches. For this, she received the National Master Craftsperson Award from the Government of India. Last year, she also received the Karnataka Rajyotsava Award.

Sandur Kushala Kala Kendra, a registered organization under the Khadi and Village Industries Commission, has adopted sustainable and local textile practices. It uses naturally available materials from the surroundings and naturally prepared dyes for fabrics. In addition to manufacturing khadi fabrics, this center also produces sugarcane and bamboo products and stone idols. Many embroidery products created by the Lambani women here have received numerous national awards, including from UNESCO.
