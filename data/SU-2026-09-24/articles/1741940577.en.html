Nestled in the lap of the Deodari mountain range in Sandur, amidst dense forests and rocky hills, lies 'Rashid Padi', a rare historical site that has recently garnered national attention due to archaeological discoveries. A small residential area amidst the hills and forests is locally known as 'Padi'. It is said that this area got the name 'Rashid Padi' in memory of a person named Rashid who once lived here.

However, this name is not just the identity of a residential area. Today, it is a symbol of living history, harboring traces of human civilization from ten thousand years ago. Based on scientific dating (Carbon Dating) tests, researchers have expressed the significant opinion that the traces of human habitation here are approximately ten thousand years old.

The curiosity, 'Is such an ancient history hidden so close to our Sandur?' motivated us to walk towards Rashid Padi. We all set out from Kottur, from Sriramasettyhalli, towards this site. As we drove towards Rashid Padi, sixteen kilometers from Sandur, the lush green Deodari mountain ranges on both sides of the road seemed to warmly welcome us. The hills, covered in a green blanket of monsoon, stood in a row, one behind the other, appearing like giant fortress walls built by nature itself.

Forest Path

The cornfields visible along the way swayed to the rhythm of the wind, creating green waves. The small villages encountered en route revealed the simple beauty of rural life. Tiled houses, cattle tied in front of houses, children laughing and playing in the street... all of this transported us to another era for a moment.

After crossing Ranjitpur, the road changed its character. The forest began to deepen. Tall trees formed green arches on both sides of the road. The chirping of birds, the rustling of leaves, the sound of the wind, and the anonymous sounds of the forest heard from afar served as a natural soundtrack to our trek. In some places, we had to proceed carefully on the rocky, uneven path. Yet, the surrounding beauty and the eagerness to see Rashid Padi made us forget the fatigue of our steps.

Finally, upon reaching near Rashid Padi, a huge rock cave, silently standing amidst nature, welcomed us. The dense greenery, cool breeze, and the profound silence that enveloped the place—all together gave the place a wonderful spiritual and historical atmosphere.

Ten Thousand Years of History

In 2024, a team of archaeological researchers scientifically examined several caves in the Sandur forest, including Rashid Padi. Microliths, human bone fragments, and animal remains were found on the floor of the cave.

Based on carbon dating tests conducted in collaboration with the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) in Ahmedabad and the Center for Archaeological Sciences at IIT Gandhinagar, researchers have opined that the traces of human habitation here are approximately ten thousand years old.

These findings suggest the significant possibility that this cave, located about 850 meters above sea level, could be one of India's oldest high-altitude human settlements.

It is estimated that the microliths found here were used by early humans for hunting, cutting meat, and other daily activities. Another special feature is that these caves were not formed by the dissolution of ordinary limestone; geologists believe they were formed by cracks caused by tectonic plate movements and the uplift of rocks.

When we stepped inside the cave, recalling all these discoveries, it was not just an ordinary trip. We were not just entering a cave; we were stepping into the memories of humans who lived ten thousand years ago.

Meanwhile, a large number of nature lovers and trekkers had arrived from distant places. The same curiosity was clearly visible on their faces as on ours.

Before us, a massive rock cave, approximately 40 feet wide and 100 feet high, amazed us with its grandeur. Inside it was another spacious cave, with enough room for people to move around easily. The images of gods enshrined on the rock there proclaimed the spiritual significance of the place.

According to locals, even today, many devotees come here during the Shravana month to offer prayers and perform bhajans. Thus, this cave remains not only an archaeological site but also a center of local belief and devotion.

Another narrow cave next to the main cave invited us to an adventure. We could walk upright for the first ten feet. Then, as the path gradually narrowed, we had to crawl backward to proceed. After a few moments of adventure, a surprisingly spacious natural chamber appeared inside, large enough for six or seven people to sit comfortably. We were amazed to see that chamber carved by nature thousands of years ago.

Further on, two more narrow caves were visible. But the sharp screeching sounds of bats emanating from them, the suffocating smell of guano spread in the air, and the porcupine quills scattered here and there warned us. Even with a torch in hand, no one dared to go further inside.

Entering this cave is not possible for everyone. Only those with slender bodies can pass through that narrow path. Every stone, every crack here seemed to tell a story. We may not be able to hear those stories completely; but we can only experience their presence from the bottom of our hearts.

To preserve Rashid Padi is not just to save a cave; it is to safely hand over ten thousand years of human history hidden in the soil of Sandur to future generations.

One does not always have to go to distant countries to find history. Sometimes, in the lap of a hill near our home, a silently standing cave waits to tell the story of thousands of years of civilization. It is a matter of pride for all of us that such a rare historical pride belongs to our Sandur's Rashid Padi. g
