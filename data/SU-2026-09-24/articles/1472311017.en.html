Roots in the slum, sprouts in 'NASA'

jayakumar4.jpg

Jayakumar Vaidya

Rahul Belagali

If you visit the Dharavi or Kurla slum areas of Mumbai, life there is astonishing. On one hand, it's about how to build a life with minimal facilities, and on the other, the small, narrow paths and tiny houses there show how to move forward while facing challenges that are not easily resolved.

Most people living in slums may not be able to live in huge bungalows. However, they build their future by living for years in houses measuring 8 to 10 feet. In a way, they subtly and indirectly convey that 'life in hardship is also beautiful. One just has to discover it.' They live it. And they remain that way.

Jayakumar Vaidya came from such a slum. Like millions of people, he too could have been content with 'this is enough for my life'. He could have been one among a thousand people, relying on daily wages. But he didn't agree to that and turned towards new ideas. That led him to become a scientist at NASA, the American space agency.

Jayakumar, who spent his childhood in the Kurla slum, never even imagined becoming a scientist one day. Growing up under the care of his mother without a father, he suffered the heat of poverty. Unable to eat full meals, he spent his days eating vada pav. Seeing all this, his neighbors mostly advised him, 'What will you do by studying? Go to work, earn money.' But neither his mother nor he liked this.

Despite financial problems and even when they had to take loans, both pledged that there was no question of deviating from education. In many situations, his mother Nalini could not pay the school fees, which ranged from ₹300 to ₹500. At such times, the school authorities would not allow Jayakumar to appear for the exams.

Amidst all this, Jayakumar, while pursuing his education, also started doing small jobs. His belief and hope were that even a small amount of money from work would provide some support for life. But his interest in science did not diminish even a little.

Eventually, he joined K.J. Somaiya College of Engineering and studied Electronics. He achieved success in the field of robotics and won awards. He got the opportunity to do an internship at Larsen & Toubro (L&T).

As days passed, he joined the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) as a researcher. He managed research, education, and family responsibilities simultaneously.

Around 2017, two of his research papers were published in international scientific journals. These piqued the interest of the University of Virginia in America. The university helped him with further studies and research. He joined there as a research assistant. Later, he joined the Goddard Space Flight Center. When he was thinking of moving on from there, NASA beckoned him.

In addition to being an engineer at NASA, Jayakumar also works at the University of Maryland in Baltimore. His main responsibility is to ensure that the scientific instruments and machines used for studying the atmospheres of Mars, the Moon, and Earth function properly. He has been awarded several prestigious awards.

Just as actor Shah Rukh Khan, who plays a NASA scientist in director Ashutosh Gowariker's film 'Swades', is drawn to his home country India, Jayakumar also has a similar desire and admiration. His aspiration is that 'India should become a self-reliant country in technology production'.

It is not enough for scientists in India to achieve success; his objective is that through them, the country should achieve unique accomplishments in the field of technology at a global level. Jayakumar is striving for this.

Poverty, lack of facilities, lack of encouragement, inferiority complex... Despite all these obstacles, Jayakumar Vaidya is an example that a goal is important to build a life and achieve success.

Jayakumar's hard work is proof that talents blossom at an international level with determination, overcoming poverty and pain. These talents take India to a different height and bring it global recognition. Jayakumar is a shining lamp for those who complain about the system despite having all conveniences.
