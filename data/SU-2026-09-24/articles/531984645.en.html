Light! Flashing light! No electricity needed! No firewood needed! No fuel needed! It won't burn your hand if you touch it! A living lantern! A magical lamp! Moving cold light! That's a firefly.

The firefly, belonging to the 'Lampyridae' family, is known by various names like firefly, glowworm, twinkling star, firefly...!

In our country, researchers have compiled a review list of reports published here and there about fireflies during the period from 1881 to 2025. A detailed research article on this has been published in the ZOOTAX research journal.

According to a 2026 study in our country, there is information about the presence of fireflies in 22 states and one union territory. The highest number of fireflies, i.e., 25.33%, are found in the Western Ghats. In our country, fireflies are found in the northeastern Gangetic plains, coastal environments, and the Deccan plateau region in numbers of 22.66%, 17.33%, and 13.33% respectively. In the northern Himalayan regions of Ladakh, Tibet, Kailash, Zanskar, 1.33% are found, and in the actual Himalayan region and islands, 2.66% of fireflies are found. There is information on 27 genera and 92 species of fireflies. Of these, 60% are native.

Globally, fireflies are found in all countries except Antarctica, including America, Canada, South America, Malaysia, Japan, Taiwan, Thailand, Philippines, Australia, and Europe. Worldwide, 90 genera and 2400 species of fireflies have been discovered. The highest number of fireflies in the world are in China! About 200 species are found there!

While there is diversity in the body color of fireflies worldwide, there is also a rare body-colored firefly. Its name is 'Blue Ghost'! It is a blue firefly. It emits dim light. It is active in May-June. It is found in the Appalachian Mountains, which stretch about 2000 miles from the eastern part of America, towards Canada and Albania. It is also found in the Bernheim forests of Kentucky.

The origin of the glow

A firefly is a small, soft-bodied insect that glows. Bioluminescent, soft-bodied winged beetle. A small lamp that glows in the dark!

According to Westerners, fireflies were created by a god named Haeres! Haeres is the father of fireflies! Haeres is the god of events that happen in the universe. According to Christian religious myths, Beelzebub is the earthly lord of fireflies, a powerful demoness, one of the seven queens in hell! Once a princess, Beelzebub, cursed by God, descends to earth as a gluttonous, corrupt, rotten representative, a demoness! That demoness rules the fireflies!

Fireflies glow from their abdomen! They have a special organ in their abdomen. It contains a chemical called 'luciferin' (color). Light is produced by the combined biochemical process of luciferase, enzyme, oxygen, and ATP fuel. It is also called nature's jewel! A twinkling star that God has brought down to earth! In scientific terms, it is the bioluminescence of fireflies!

Beliefs surrounding fireflies

Children are overjoyed to see the glow of fireflies! In Western cultures, it is believed that fireflies are a ray of hope that guides lost children in the forest and brings them back safely! According to various mythological and folk cultures worldwide, if fireflies are seen, it is a sign of good luck, prosperity, and a good future! According to Western culture, if fireflies are twinkling in the sky at night, it is a Christian religious message that 'everything is alright, have firm faith in God, you are walking on the path of righteousness, you will shine like light until the end of time'! Some Western tribes also have superstitions about fireflies. If fireflies appear, misfortune awaits! It means someone in the house will die.

According to Lynn Faust, a firefly expert in Tennessee, USA, the flash emitted by fireflies is a love song of a mating partner! An invitation to love! In reality, it is a unique information communication process! A warning in dangerous situations, a self-defense indicator. They also use their flash as a dangerous weapon to scare away predators, indicating that their body is not tasty to eat and is poisonous!

Life cycle...

Fireflies are mostly seen two months before the onset of the rainy season. They live in warm climates, wetlands, grasslands, forests, caves, bushes, coastal areas, and trees. Their natural lifespan in forests is only two months! The female firefly lays 500 eggs in moist soil. Fireflies do not bite or sting humans! They are harmless insects! They do not damage farmers' crops.

Larvae are gluttons. No matter how much they eat, it's not enough. Snails, slugs, earthworms, insects, and bugs... these are their food. Along with eating, they also store enough food, or fuel, in their bodies to eat when they grow up. Adult fireflies generally do not eat. Even if they do, it's a small amount. Especially during mating with female insects. They specifically eat flower nectar.

Adult fireflies live by using the stored fuel produced from the food they ate as larvae. The function of adult fireflies is dedicated to reproduction.

Male fireflies fly. Female fireflies cannot fly. They do not have wings. Even if they do, they are short. They are not useful for flying. Therefore, females stay on the ground. At night, they climb to the tips of grass blades, or crawl up small plant branches, and flash to call their male lovers.

Fireflies on the verge of extinction

Fireflies are on the verge of extinction. They are in a dangerous situation. Light pollution, artificial light, habitat destruction, excessive use of pesticides, climate change, and predation by animals have threatened their life cycle. Due to these reasons, the number of fireflies is decreasing year by year.

A firefly conservation and research organization was founded by firefly expert Ben Pfeiffer in Texas, USA, in 1909. The main objectives are to conserve fireflies and conduct research on them.

In the food industry...

In the food industry, fireflies are used as an indicator to assess whether food is fit for consumption or spoiled. If fireflies land on food, it means it is pure and fit for consumption!

If they move away without landing, it indicates that the food is not fit for consumption. It indicates that the food is contaminated with toxic microorganisms such as bacteria, mold, and fungi. Fireflies, especially the larvae, detect the smell of chemicals like geosmin released by microorganisms in food from a distance through their highly sensitive sensory organs and move away from the food!
