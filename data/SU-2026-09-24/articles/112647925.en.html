Do women fall for deceivers easily?

Serial 17

I told Prabhakar about Suvarnasundari. 'All this is common these days. You are an old-fashioned girl. Take it lightly. Anyway, I'm going to interview Kuppuswamy. Are you coming? If you bring a camera, you can also interview,' he said.

'I should ask our channel,' I said, calling them, 'Our people just interviewed him the other day. If you want, I'll just come with you.'

The interview lasted about two hours. He struggled a lot to answer some questions. Sukumar came in the middle of the interview. He smiled as if he recognized me. Although I turned my face away, I was surprised that he remembered me even after all these days, just by seeing me once. Only when Prabhakar introduced me as his future wife did he look at me in a certain way.

After both of them came out, he asked, 'Do you know that Sukumar?'

'Both of you were introduced to me at the same time,' I said like a suspense writer. As I expected, he asked in surprise, 'Both at the same time?'

'You first met me at a clothing store, didn't you? He was there that day too.' Saying this, I told him everything that happened with Amritavarshini.

'I know a lot about him. His father is involved in a big liquor scam. Now, I went there under the pretext of an interview for that very reason. I'm preparing a good article. He won't escape. Straight to jail,' he said.

'Why bother with that minister? Leave it,' I said, scared.

'You're talking like a perfect girl.'

'No, I'm talking as your future wife.'

'That's the job I do. As a journalist, how can I be afraid to expose this injustice? I am not a reporter. I am a journalist.'

'What's the difference?'

'A reporter is someone who brings news. A journalist is someone who seeks the truth.'

'Do all journalists do that?'

'No. They take money from five thousand to fifty lakhs to expose mistakes. Some newspapers and channels encourage them by saying, 'We can't pay salaries. If you don't want the collected news to be printed, take money there.' Can you imagine how far these journalists' threats go? In these fifty years, nine journalists have become Rajya Sabha members. They are big shots. Only one of them caught a party scam and threatened the government. The party in power then made him a Rajya Sabha member. That's how things work at a high level! I believe that if at least some people like me fight, the journalism profession will gain respect.'

I had never seen him talk so seriously. It's true that I was a little scared. He noticed it and, to make me normal, he laughed and said, 'Since you are going to marry me, I'm telling you beforehand that we should never have differences of opinion on this matter.'

I also changed the topic and said, 'Forget about that minister. I'm talking about his son.'

'He's like that. He can't resist a churidar, and he won't leave a saree alone. He traps them in some way.'

'Although that's not surprising, my friend's situation seems strange to me. Until then, she had cursed him badly, but as soon as he offered a lift, she found out he was the minister's son and went with him in the car.'

'You don't know much about the world. People are like that. Four friends sit together and joke about a fifth person who isn't there. As soon as the fifth person arrives, they laugh and invite him. The same person who cursed an actor on Facebook until then, if he accidentally meets that celebrity, he struggles and begs for a photo. Except for staunch communists and honest poets, people don't have clear ideologies or distinct likes and dislikes.'

'Since the topic came up, I'm asking. You don't have any objection to me being active on Facebook, do you?' I asked.

'Objection? Why?' he asked in surprise.

'Bhanu Annayya didn't like it. That's why.'

''There's nothing like that. Many young writers, male and female, and for that matter, even experienced writers, use Facebook to promote their books. If four people appreciate what you've written, that encouragement will motivate you to write even better. You'll get to know people with deep knowledge of the subject. If there's a mistake in what you thought was 'very well written,' they'll point it out. You'll understand your mistakes. The arrogance of 'I am great' will diminish.''

I remembered Suvarna's review of my post and said, 'What you said is true.'

''Of course. Assume that some people on social media are jobless. These pseudo-intellectuals, who believe that 'the silence of intellectuals is a social betrayal,' will satirically criticize your opinions if they are contrary to theirs. There are also some rotten eggplants. They constantly curse badly without manners. But you have to tolerate it. In a way, it's good. Your skin will toughen up that way. You'll gain the honesty to accept strong criticism and the courage to endure weak criticism. Couples post photos of themselves kissing and hugging to show that they are happy. Another person thinks that if he doesn't tell the world that he has been suffering from indigestion for four days, that Berg will be sad. Overall, Facebook is useful for all these different kinds of attitudes and analyses. If used correctly, it's nectar; otherwise, it's poison,'' he encouraged.

The disappointment I felt when Bhanumurthy Annayya said no disappeared with these words. Don't we prefer those who say what we 'like' rather than what is 'good' for us? In that way, Facebook became an addiction for me. Did it become poison for my life? Or nectar? That's another matter.

The likes and appreciation I received started giving me good encouragement. Elders say that structure is important for a story and style for an article, don't they? I got a little used to that. From that, I started posting small poems. That's when a funny incident happened.

Our channel sent someone to interview a poet. They say he recites impromptu poems. Before going for the interview, we learn about him and write down some questions.

When the interview was halfway through, I asked, 'They say you have a lot of ego, is that true?' He seemed hurt. That's what our channel wanted. That's why we ask such questions. With sadness, he said, 'If some people consider my self-confidence as ego, that's their mistake.'

'They say you believe whatever you believe is the Veda,' I provoked further.

'Anyone believes their own belief is true,' he said passionately.

The interview went on like this for about two hours. As I adjusted the camera and was about to leave, I asked him, 'Will you recite an impromptu poem for me? I'll post it on Facebook as if I wrote it myself. Please.' I took out a paper and pen and asked like a small child.

'The pair of breasts of a woman flaunt with arrogance, and with pride, they swell like a mountain king,' he immediately recited impromptu. I don't know what 'kuchayugma' means. In fact, I didn't understand a single bit of this poem. Still, I posted it on Facebook as if it were my own. That became my undoing. I will explain in detail how that happened later.

***

Prabhakar took me with him to interview a lawyer. They say he is like Palkhiwala for the Telugu state, and his opinions on God are very interesting. 'It would be good if you also came,' he said and took me. That was not the real reason. We were both in a state where we couldn't bear to be without each other. Isn't it deep love when one person's company is good for the other?

In the interview, Prabhakar also mentioned Minister Kuppuswamy's name. I understood why he was interviewing this lawyer, leaving other work.

'The minister is caught in a big scam. You are his lawyer. Don't you feel guilty?' he asked. It was a very tricky question. I thought the lawyer would get angry. But he didn't.

'No one is guilty until proven,' he said.

'But you know in your heart, don't you?'

'Karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana. It is important to do one's duty. We are lawyers.'

'On one hand, you talk about Krishna and the Bhagavad Gita. On the other hand, you say there is no God. Are you Goebbels?'

'Meaning?'

'A cat on the wall.'

Even to that, he didn't get angry. I admired his composure. He explained his opinion about God with a good example, 'An intellectual says that if you immerse yourself on such a day, at such a time, in such a place, you will gain merit.' 'Atheists say that three hundred people die in stampedes at Pushkar every year across India.' 'A devotee says that if you anoint a Shiva lingam with milk, all difficulties will be removed.' An atheist says, 'If anointing with milk removes all difficulties, then no one in this world should have any difficulties, right?' We say, 'Isn't that devotee getting temporary relief?' Our opposition is not to devotees. It is to self-proclaimed intellectuals.

'Who are they?'

'Our opposition is to those who attract innocent devotees with pseudo-scientific reasons, saying that the oxygen produced when carbohydrates in milk touch the stone during the anointing of a Shiva lingam with milk is good for health, and then extort money from them. That's why Vemana said, 'No matter how much knowledge one gains, if one doesn't listen to anything, one will not give up one's bad qualities. If you wash charcoal in milk, will it become white, O Vishwadaabhirama, listen, Vema?''

Hearing him recite the poem, Prabhakar pointed to me next to him and said, 'This is the girl I'm marrying. A small-sized poet,' and turning to me, he said, 'Recite the poem you wrote the other day.'

When he asked so suddenly, I was startled. Although I didn't remember the poem, fortunately, I had the paper where I had written it down when the poets recited it. I showed it to them. As they read, their eyebrows furrowed.

'Did you write this yourself?' they asked. I said yes.

'What kind of poems are these at this age, dear?' they asked.

'What happened to that poem? What did she write?' Prabhakar asked eagerly.

'I don't know much Sanskrit. I understood bits and pieces. Your future wife will become a great poet in the future,' they said. The signs of a tsunami appeared in the distance. It felt like something was shaking somewhere.

'What did she write?' Prabhakar asked.

'Kuchayugma means breasts, Giripati means the king of mountains, Himalaya. I think this poem is about women with elevated breasts. I understood that much. Is that right, dear?' they asked, turning to me.

It felt like slipping on an egg in front of boys in the college canteen. By then, my throat was dry, and I didn't say anything else. Noticing my distress, Prabhakar saved me by saying, 'We'll leave now, Anand. Thanks for the interview.'

When I came home and looked at Facebook, my head spun. It had already been a day since I posted the impromptu poem given by the poet. I didn't know about the commotion that had been going on behind it since yesterday. Many people were already attacking me with comments, like Abhimanyu caught in the Padmavyuha.

'This poem is a small part of what appears in Vijayavilasa. This lady has added some of her own and written it as hers,' someone posted. I was surprised. Did the poets who recited this poem to me always recite poems in public impromptu by copying some parts from somewhere and adding a little of their own? Or did they take revenge like this, angered by my questions about their ego?

Whatever it was, I was the one who suffered the loss. Even though I deleted the post, some people had already taken screenshots, so it became global. The waves kept hitting.

'This girl, who developed the habit of copying at a young age, can soon shine as a director in the film industry,' someone commented. 'She will soon become a great poet,' another commented.

'Her age is twenty-three in her profile. Is there a need for a discussion about hair at this age?' another person scolded. My heart stopped. There was no mention of hair anywhere in the poem. What was this then? Thinking this, I searched for Vijayavilasam on Google. I found this poem. There was also a summary. As I read it, my palms sweated.

Gada, the brother of Krishna and Balarama, describes Subhadra's beauty to Arjuna in this way: 'Her newly rising breasts mock the Himalayas with pride. The flaunting hair on her navel, which was not there at birth, now swells with the beauty that came with this tender youth, showing arrogance and pride. When unexpected wealth comes, some people in the world are arrogant like this, aren't they?' the poet wrote. Reading that, I was distraught, not knowing where to hide my head.

'A writer wrote this in his novel three decades ago,' commented an employee of the Archaeological Survey of India.

'Oh, not just the poem, even the feeling is copied. My finger in her nose,' someone wrote. I looked at the name. It said 'Save India.'

''For a girl who has come of age, 'youth' becomes pimples on her cheeks, and for some, it becomes poetry on their breasts. Get married as soon as possible, mother,'' another person wrote, adding a heart emoji.

'Even if a sweet lime grows big, it becomes a pomelo. Then it becomes a jackfruit,' said a scoundrel who had not drunk his mother's milk.

I was only less sad. If I had been hurt earlier, Bhanu Annayya would have scolded me, saying, 'That's why I told you not to use Facebook or Nosebook.'

At that time, Prabhakar was my support!

'I told you before. All this is common, take it lightly. Such experiences are lessons that books don't teach,' he said. I was happy. If the boy you are marrying is someone who can comfort you, what more could a beautiful Mahalakshmi want than that?'

Suvarna also comforted me in the same way. Look, someone wrote, 'Are her breasts sweet limes? Or pomelos?' Don't be sad. Pity him. Fights at home, disappointment, dissatisfaction with children - all these things turn them into negative thinkers. They vent all their frustration on us as if they have found a weapon. If someone writes something wrong, it can be said normally. But they write as if they have caught a big mistake, sarcastically, ironically, and in disgusting language, forgetting decency,' she said.

'Some of my friends... have sent me those comments on WhatsApp, saying, 'Have you seen how badly this person has written about you?'' I said.

'These are even worse. Not just anyone. Your friends! If anyone writes something bad about you anywhere, they forward it to you. They hurt your feelings. If you tell them that, they justify it by saying, 'I sent it so you would know what people think about you.' They hide their sadism under the guise of well-wishers.'

Her words made me feel completely normal again, and I thanked her wholeheartedly.

'What is this poem really about?' she asked. Embarrassed, I said, 'I know 'lalane' means woman. I didn't know 'kuchayugma' means breasts. I thought it was a poem about the virtues women should have,' and told her everything that happened.

She laughed for a long time and then said, 'Sorry.' I said, 'It's all right.'

'We've become so close, haven't we? Since this incident has come up, I'll ask you a question. Don't mind it. What's your breast size?'

Startled by her sudden question, I replied, 'I don't like such topics, sorry.'

'I don't like it either. I asked because we're close. It reminded me of someone asking if they were sweet limes or pomelos. There's another reason for asking this.'

What is it?

I feel very shy to go out without a veil. Forty-two at such a young age. It's even harder if it rains. That's why I asked about yours, I have no other intention.

I said, hurt, 'Oh... that's not my intention. All this is new to me! That's why I was confused. If yours is one problem, mine is another kind. Twenty-four. Compared to other girls, I shrink with an inferiority complex. I envy people like you.'

'Envy? Why? Some doctor said in an interview that some people with large breasts have a chance of getting back pain after forty. I read in Vatsyayana's book that people with small breasts have a good sex life. Congratulations.'

Since Sundari's words made me happy, I thanked her and said with more familiarity, 'For those who don't have problems, this seems like a very small matter. But you'll understand if you experience it.'
