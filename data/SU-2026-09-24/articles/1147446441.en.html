Vasudeva Sharma N.V.

School and college education is essential these days. Individuals who have received education develop the skill to obtain necessary information from various sources. They also gain the ability to claim and assert their rights, and to identify and prevent injustice. Education is an indicator of development for an individual, family, society, village, and even the country.

The Indian Constitution, in Article 21(A), mandates that 'the right to education for all children' must be compulsorily provided. In Article 51(A)(K), the Constitution also directs that it is the duty of parents to compulsorily involve their children in education.

For this purpose, the Government of India has implemented the 'Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act (RTE), 2009'. The responsibility for its implementation rests with local, state, and central governments. This means that governments must make the school system attractive and effective to prevent any girl or boy from being deprived of education, dropping out of education to become child laborers, becoming victims of child marriage, becoming mothers in childhood, or becoming orphans and begging. No child can be denied school admission based on caste, religion, gender, disability, economic status, language, or any other background. Every child must be involved in education, ensuring that they do not develop an inferiority complex. This right to education means that all schools, whether government or non-government, must comply with all provisions of the RTE Act.

The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 envisions that all children in India should be in learning for 15 years [Anganwadi + 1st-2nd grade from 3 to 8 years; 3rd-5th grade from 8 to 11 years; 6th-8th grade from 11 to 14 years; and 9th-12th grade from 14 to 18 years]. After this, there are opportunities for 3 to 4 years of undergraduate degrees, 1 to 2 years of postgraduate degrees, and further education.

Education liberates everyone from slavery, bondage, humiliation, discrimination, and hardships, and guides them towards a scientific outlook, justice, and logical thinking, and development.
