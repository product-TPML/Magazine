Festivals are for uniting

Victory

Rashmi S

In Maharashtra, during this year's Navratri, the Vishwa Hindu Parishad has issued a directive stating that Muslims should be kept out of Garbha dance.

On this occasion, Maharashtra Minister Nitesh Rane has also supported the Vishwa Hindu Parishad's move, stating that there is a high possibility of 'Love Jihad'. However, it is not right to exclude a community from celebrations based on religion during festivals that are celebrated with harmony as their essence.

Among the many Navratri celebrations, Garbha and Dandiya dances hold a prominent place. Today, these two dances have crossed the borders of Gujarat and are popular wherever 'Gujjubhai' (Gujarati brothers or siblings) are present. In these dances of Devi worship, Garbha symbolizes the cycle of life. Dandiya symbolizes the victory of good over evil. Sticks are used instead of swords. In Garbha, people dance in a circle, clapping their hands.

Both these dances are also included in UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage. This tradition, where people used to mingle and dance with their neighbors, now sees all enthusiasts gather in pandals to dance, eat, and worship the Goddess. Amidst the sounds of DJs, the frenzy of Bollywood songs has also increased. The celebration often seems to reach new heights, surpassing devotion.

In these communal dances, steps speak beyond caste, status, color, and body shape. By dancing together, they strengthen their bonds.

In the multifaceted celebrations of pluralistic India, this includes everyone. In such a celebration, Muslims are not allowed to participate. Those participating in Devi worship are being restricted, being asked to bring their identity cards and recite Hanuman Chalisa.

Music and dance have no religious boundaries. If worshipped through fine arts, then everything else should be secondary to art. Indian culture is about inclusion, not exclusion and celebration.

Setting aside cultural heritage, what does the law of the land say? Our constitution grants equal rights to all. Moreover, it does not validate discrimination based on caste and religion. The Vishwa Hindu Parishad should understand the country's constitution before making such a decision. Political leaders should also not insult the constitution for their vote bank. Nitesh Rane should not make such inappropriate statements.

During celebrations, ensuring the safety of women and preventing harassment should be paramount. Attention should be paid to restricting DJs and giving more importance to traditional songs and dances. These songs and dances, which are links to our intangible cultural heritage, should not be relegated to the background amidst the frenzy of film songs and steps.

In the light of lamps, with an orchestra and singers, this dance was performed by placing a lamp symbolizing the sanctum sanctorum in front of the team. They danced in circles, symbolizing the cycle of life such as birth and death, and the revolution of the earth. The protection of culture should come from the protection of this art form. This folk art needs to be preserved from the extravagant dances that happen in its name. Even now, traditional groups should be identified and given more importance. Noise pollution and light pollution should be prevented. When this art and celebration are preserved, Indian culture will be safe. Dividing and ruling, and sowing seeds of poison among people, will not protect any culture.
