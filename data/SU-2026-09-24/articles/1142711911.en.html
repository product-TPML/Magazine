Road Trip of Celebrity Chickens

Art: Santhosh Sasihithlu

Suma Ramesh
Art: Santhosh Sasihithlu

A resident of a prestigious locality in Bengaluru brought two country chickens from their farmhouse. Within a couple of weeks, this pair was reserved to host relatives who were coming from abroad. The hen, which had a zero figure, and the moderately plump rooster, had to accumulate at least four-packs, if not six-packs, within that limited period. Thus, special care began for the poultry pair. Along with rice, ragi, pulses, they consumed all the food supplied to them, including dry fruits, pizza, burgers, French fries, noodles, pasta... and became well-fed in just four days.

Just as "the fatter the sheep, the more profit for the shepherd," the family rejoiced. Meanwhile, whether it was the misfortune of the foreign friends or the luck of the chickens, their trip was canceled due to unavailability of leave. Coincidentally, at the same time, an occasion arose to perform Satyanarayana Swami Puja at the house where the chickens were residing. The priests, who performed the puja with devotion, issued a non-vegetarian ban in the house for 48 days. This brought good fortune and good times for the chickens.

The compound of the house where the chickens had migrated was spacious. Many ornamental plants were planted in pots all around. In the beginning, the chickens, which used to climb and jump on the pots, scratch the soil, and defecate here and there, completely changed after experiencing the corporate lifestyle of the metropolis. Since the surrounding compound was very high, they stood near the gate in the middle and watched the city life. They observed and understood everything – the vehicles constantly moving, modernly dressed people, their manners, signal problems, delivery boys, restaurants – and in a few days, they became complete 'Bangaloreans'. They changed their walking style, which was previously haphazard. The way they turned their necks, flapped their wings, and took steps changed. Without bothering the potted plants or even touching the soil, they walked only on the tiles, keeping their bodies and minds clean.

They didn't copy 'catwalk' but brought many variations to 'cock walk' and showed originality. Overall, the poultry, accustomed to the Bangalore climate, became completely groomed.

In fifteen days, the pair, having patrolled the entire boundary of the house and grown tired of seeing the city only through the gate, jumped the compound and stepped onto the road, desiring to roam outside. Initially startled by the huge traffic, they later learned to weave through it and move forward. Bengaluru city became a paradise for them. Although the family was anxious about the missing chickens, they were relieved when they returned after their road trip by evening. Within a month, this pair became well-known in the surrounding streets.

For Bangaloreans, who had already seen sparrows disappear, crows vanish, and only pigeons remaining, these vintage chickens with red and black feathers seemed unique. People began to stop and observe them as if it were a rare sight. When they crossed the road, even fast-moving vehicles would brake. Those exhausted from night shifts, those heartbroken from breakups, those whose faces were pale from stress, would relax their brows and smile faintly when they saw the chickens. Apart from ambulances, only they had the freedom to jump signals. Gen Alpha children, who had never seen a live chicken before, would stand on the road and ask questions appropriate for their generation: 'Is it real or AI?', 'Is it a robot?', 'Which company manufactured it? Where do they put the battery in it?'

Their parents would search through all the junk files in their brains and finally say, 'Yes, that is real.' Senior citizens out for a walk would exclaim, 'Oh, how freely these chickens roam even in such traffic, this... this... is nature!' and rejoice. Another child of the new generation there would ask, 'Is the Nature app available on the Play Store?' and search on their mobile.

Unaware of all this, the freely roaming chicken couple would cock-walk in front of fruit shops, restaurants, and malls, while fans would swarm like flies, some offering grains, some taking selfies, some gently stroking them. The slightly shy hen would hesitate here and there, pecking at its beak in front of a beauty salon. The rooster, however, strutted around like an unofficial corporator of that prestigious locality. Those who were blessed to take selfies with the chickens uploaded them on social media, further increasing their fan following. The corporator there was astonished to see that these chickens were getting the name and fame that he, despite serving for forty years in Bengaluru's traffic jams, road accidents, flooded roads during rains, and times of excessive and insufficient rainfall, had not received.

Meanwhile, even after the forty-eight-day non-vegetarian ban ended, it did not seem like the end for the chickens. Their lifeline had extended, and they had attained a lifelong period of good fortune. The celebrity charisma of the chickens led to an increase in visitors, and the family grew tired. They wondered if they should release them back into the farm.

That day, the main agenda for 'Chai, Lunch, and Dinner Pe Charcha' became this. The chickens, who had already adapted to the Bengaluru lifestyle, watched the proceedings of the meeting from behind the scenes, saying it was impossible for them to go back to their old farm. Just then, the foreign guests on the waiting list called.

The chickens were all ears. After exchanging pleasantries, when they informed that their leave had been approved and they would be arriving soon, the chickens' hearts sank. The family rejoiced at the happiness of finding an easy solution to the problem. In the middle of the conversation, as if remembering something, the guests said, 'Yes... I heard there is a celebrity chicken near your house, we would love to see it when we visit.' The family was stunned, while the chickens, who were roaming nearby, began to
