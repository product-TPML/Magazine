Interview with Sarod maestro Shiraz Ali Khan

'Music is not technique, but the exploration and expression of truth'

ustad siraz ali khan

Ustad Allauddin Khan (in the middle)

Sumangala

One of the rare phenomena that occurred in the Hindustani music field in the twentieth century was the emergence of the Maihar Gharana. Ustad Allauddin Khan, famously known as 'Boru Baba', joined the court of the Rampur king before independence, settled in Maihar, and began spreading the nectar of music. Baba, who played two hundred instruments including Sarod, Sitar, Surabahar, Surasingar, Violin, and Western instruments, also founded the 'Maihar Band', which is an exciting story. Baba's eldest son, Ustad Ali Akbar Khan, was the guru of Pt. Rajeev Taranath, the first great Sarod player of South India. Guruma Vidushi Annapurna Devi, known as the 'legend of Surabahar', Pt. Ravi Shankar, Pt. Nikhil Banerjee, Pt. Timirbaran Ghosh, Pt. Pannalal Ghosh, Vidushi Sharan Rani, Vidushi Amira Pareer, Vidushi Shishirakona Das, Ustad Ashish Khan, each of these students taught by Baba are invaluable gems of music. Another revolution Baba silently brought about was opening the doors of the Gharana, which until then taught only family members, to everyone, irrespective of caste, religion, or gender. Baba played the Sarod wonderfully. Since he played with his left hand, his brother had specially made a Sarod for him. After Baba's demise, his great-grandson Shiraz Ali Khan (son of Ustad Dhyanesh Khan) took up that Sarod, which was in Maihar's 'Madina Mandir'. Shiraz Ali Khan continues the musical tradition on this Sarod, which is about 150 years old. Here is a conversation with him, held recently when he came to Bangalore for a concert.
