B.M. Bharathi Hadige

B.M. Bharathi Hadige Hassan, Gujjar

It was eleven o'clock when I finished class and came to the staffroom. The college had just started after the holidays. Perhaps because we had stood and talked for two hours, I just wanted to sit down. During this period, students and lecturers have a fifteen-minute break. Those who want to drink coffee can drink. Or they can sit and talk. They can read the newspaper. It's up to them. In the middle of the conversation, my colleague Raviprakash said, 'Last year, we staff didn't go anywhere. This year, we should at least go for a one-day picnic.' When Shilpa madam said, 'If we're going, we should go now, sir. If syllabus, tests, evaluations, cultural-sports, and all that start, we won't be able to go anywhere,' he turned to me and said, 'Okay, let's go. Can we go next Sunday? Madam, where should we go?' Since Kuvempu's house in Shivamogga was on my mind, I suggested that. Everyone agreed without a second thought. Everyone should come near the college at five in the morning next Sunday. Let's take breakfast from here. We can go to a hotel for lunch. We will see almost all the places we find along the way and return to Hassan by eight in the night. One important thing is that no one should bring children. Because even if one child comes from one house, that's twenty children, just imagine how difficult it would be to manage them. And so on, he said. I said okay. But Jayalakshmi madam, a history lecturer, came near my ear and said, 'I'll leave my elder son at the neighbor's house. I'll bring my younger son with me. How can I leave both of them at someone else's house, madam?' I said, 'Yes, yes.'

I was happy that the time for my long-cherished wish to come true was approaching. I woke up at exactly four in the morning without them knowing, finished bathing and all, got dressed, and was about to leave. Just then, the child woke up and said, 'I want clothes, I want clothes.' My plan to open the door silently and leave was of no use. I said, 'Stay with daddy, I'll be back soon, I'll bring breakfast, you're a good boy,' and so on. Nothing worked. I had broken the rule that children should not be brought, and I was apprehensive about what others would say. But no one said anything. It hadn't even been half an hour since we left Hassan when a cold breeze blew, and a drizzle started. One person said, 'We thought there would be no rain, but this happened,' and another said, 'It's the rainy season, let it rain.' Talking about this and that, we reached Kandya. There is a temple there. Since the Bhadra river flows nearby, we sat on the bank and started eating breakfast. Jayalakshmi madam's five-year-old son ate three idlis and one uddina vada and reached for the fourth idli. 'Hey, that's enough, look Jayashree, he doesn't eat at home, but when he comes out, he eats so much, what will people think? I feel very embarrassed,' she said. 'Let him eat, madam, what do children know? You be happy. Some children don't eat at all, they cry, they throw tantrums. Let him eat,' she said and went to wash her hands. Ravi, who overheard their conversation, said, 'Take another idli, no one will say anything. There's a saying in our area, 'Rain should come, food should be eaten,' or something like that, I don't remember it correctly.' I, who was struggling to make the child eat at least half an idli, said, 'It's not like that, Ravi, 'If the son eats, there is no harm, but if it rains, there is no harm.''

The journey began amidst discussions about the proverb for a minute or two, how it is in our area, in your area, the name of rain should not be there, this proverb is not right, etc. The rain was coming and going. Everyone had brought various snacks. We sang and listened to songs. Kavimane Kavishaila was heaven to our eyes. After returning from there, I felt like being silent rather than talking.

After this, that is, on Saturday, my child and I left for our village. The reason was that I had to tell my mother, who had not traveled the country or read books, about the college picnic. Somehow, I squeezed into the bus and looked around for a seat. On one side, someone was sitting looking out the window. I went near them and asked if anyone was coming. They turned and looked and said, 'Oh, come on. No one is coming. You've gained weight, give me the child. Sit comfortably,' and our Omkaramma teacher patted my back. I greeted her with great hesitation and sat next to her and asked, 'Teacher, how are the master and son?' 'They are fine, dear. The master retired. The son is in Mangaluru. Now he is a doctor. Even after he grew up, he used to eat a lot, wherever he went, he would just eat and eat, it was very annoying for me. And then there's your proverb. Everyone who comes home tells proverbs. Be quiet, teacher, let him eat his fill. If the son eats, there is no harm. But if it rains, there is no harm, I used to get angry. Now when I see him, I feel sorry for him. He eats a little standing up. He says, 'I don't feel like eating, mom.' That's the sadness, I can't go there and stay with him. When I tell him to get married, he says, 'Wait a minute, mom.' He's very intelligent. He looks good too. He's a good son, but he doesn't eat much,' she said.

The teacher was much better than my mother. When I was in seventh grade, my friends talked about an earthquake happening, that the earth would shake. I was very scared. I went straight home and asked my mother if it was true that the earth would shake and an earthquake would happen. My mother said that Mother Earth has four sons. They carry the earth on their shoulders and stand day and night. When one shoulder gets tired, the earth shakes a little when they switch to the other shoulder. There's no problem with that. This happens once in many years. I remembered now that she said, 'What's there to be afraid of all this, child?'

My mother knows that the one who left his mark on the country with his name was a son, Bharata. But she doesn't know which Bharata. The son of Aditirthankara Rishabhadeva, Bharata, had a gentle heart. When Bahubali renounced the palace, he was immersed in a sea of sorrow, crying, 'Siriye sudu rajya padavi mahakashta ha ha sude sudenuta.' In the epic 'Bharatesha Vaibhava,' Ratnakaravarni made Bharata immortal. And when King Dushyanta was chasing a deer in the forest, he heard an ethereal voice saying, 'If you spare this deer now, you will be blessed with a son.' As he moved forward, he heard the sweet words of a maiden. The union of their unusual friendship, Gandharva marriage, separation, curse, forgetfulness, rejection, birth of a son, reunion of husband, wife, and son. One day, he became the emperor. Bharata, the good son of Shakuntala, the mother of nature. There is another one, a favorite son of many mothers. Kaikeyi is said to have gone straight to heaven. When asked why she, who was responsible for Rama's exile, went to heaven, Yama is said to have said, 'She was a king to a king, a yogi to a yogi, a devotee to a devotee, a renunciate to a renunciate, a warrior to a warrior, the mother of a brave son Bharata. Her hundred mistakes were negated by her son's single good quality. Therefore, she went to heaven.'

The folk tales woven by the words of Bhutera warm the body. 'The birth of a woman without children, I cannot bear the suffering, give me a child, Shiva,' prayed and received the son Parashurama. He is accused of being a son without a father. What would Yellamma say to the question 'Where is father?' She said a tiger ate him. He went to the ghat, killed the tigers, and asked, 'Show me which tiger killed my father.' She said it was not a tiger, but a snake. He caught the snakes and brought them and asked, 'Which snake is it?' She said it was not a snake, but people. He had enough. He pushed his mother from the ghat and set her on fire. He came straight home, hungry, eyes dark, and looked towards the kitchen. Yellamma was cooking for her son. He bowed at her feet and said, 'Forgive me, mother.' The story continues like this.
I didn't even realize we had reached Ballupete, dear. Are you thinking about something? It's getting dark, and it's raining. You're going alone, be careful, come home, you know Santepete. The child is asleep. You shouldn't give money to sleeping children, she said, giving me two hundred rupees and a biscuit packet, and said, 'Shall I go? You used to sing so well, dear, 'My son was born, a son was born to me.' It sounds like I heard it yesterday or the day before.' Saying this, she stroked the child's cheek and patted my back, saying, 'Be careful again, dear,' and the teacher got off the bus and left. I looked at her for a long time, turning around. That was the last time I saw her.
It was true that I was singing the song 'My son was born.' Along with that, I was also singing, 'Within a year of the daughter-in-law coming, my son became a stranger, infatuated by her beauty, he forgot his mother's neck. The son who forgot to say 'mother' calls her an old woman. When fate is bad, what can happen to anyone? God gave him to me for the sins of some birth.' I didn't mention it so that the teacher wouldn't feel sad. I was sitting and thinking about this and that. The bus was going on its way. Sakleshpur hadn't even arrived yet. I wondered when I would reach my village.

Naturally, boys have a greater appetite. They eat their fill. For a mother, no matter how much her son eats, it's always less. Some fathers, however, would shout for no reason. They would say whatever came to their mouths.
The monkey that ate the rice became lazy, the lazy one roamed the house, ate, and sat. Look at his chest, look at his arms, look at his thighs, how he is like Bhima, his only job is to eat and grow, he only ate what he went around and ate, then they would mock him, 'Chew, cut, carry, graze cattle, collect firewood, leaves,' no matter what he did, not a single good word would come from the fathers' mouths. Whatever happened in the house, the son was blamed for it. In a time when mothers' lives were burning with fire, it was not said that the son suffered. The son is not one who walked on a bed of flowers. If there were no sons in the house, women were like trash. If they were born, they should not eat their fill. In the time of mothers' suffering, the painful voice 'If the son eats, there is no harm, but if it rains, there is no harm' might have originated. It might have been the reason for the birth of the saying. If you look at it, there are not one, two, three, four proverbs. There are thousands. In 1852, the first collection of Kannada proverbs was published in Mangaluru. It collected 3,457 proverbs. In 1874, the Basel Mission also worked to collect thousands of proverbs. Mysore University has conducted studies on the origin, nature, and value of proverbs. N. Tapaswikumar is its
surveyor.

Even today, there are mothers who tell a proverb for every word. By the way, who is a son? He who is a king in conduct, a lord in morality. He who built lakes and dug wells. He who does not abandon those who trust him, a son is one who is committed to the lines 'Protect the word, protect the border, protect the mother, or die.' A son is one who touches the soil, cultivates, and provides food. A son is one who lends a shoulder in times of difficulty. A son is one who protects the country, abandoning the ties of life and emotion. He should eat his fill. A son means trust. A son means self-confidence. A son means courage. The Someshwara Shataka clarifies that one who is immoral and acts immorally is not a son. The rain should fall on the soil where the footprints of the great mothers who gave birth to such people and celebrated the affection of motherhood are imprinted. At this moment, I thought like this.

The bus reached Sakleshpur. I slowly got off the bus. It was already seven o'clock. By the time I caught another bus and reached home, it would be nine o'clock. The bus stand, my fate, the sleeping child. With a bag in my hand, I came and sat down somewhere, getting wet. The bus had to come on the road.
According to Roman mythology, June is an auspicious month. Juno is the name of the queen of heaven. She is like a mother who protects marriage. Those who believe this get married in this month. At the time when special flowers like Brahmakamala, Jasmine, Chrysanthemum, and Water Lily bloom, the great nature's replicas, the mountain peaks of Malnad and Sahyadri, celebrate by bathing in the rain. The eyes of the earth bloom with joy. To tell the truth, this is the time to open the eyes of our inner self.
Without rain, there is no earth, no crops, no life. Not only that, there are no movies. No music. No poets. Monsoon rain, rain came, rain. Come, magical rain, come, rain, it has rained again, raindrops of Swati pearl, look at the rain, not one or two, but hundreds. By the way, there are twenty-seven types of rain in total. The current number of rains is sixteen. Adra, Aridra, Adri, this is the sixth rain. Each rain is said to have a deity. Adri rain is said to fall in the name of Mahārudra, and it must fall because the temple is in Kesavala, near Mudigere, which is my mother's native place.
The bus came on the road as if asking whether to come or not, and stopped as if asking whether to stop or not. Somehow, getting wet in the rain, I got on the bus and sat down. My child, who was sleeping and repeatedly asking if grandma's house had come, was now fully awake and started yawning. I got off the bus, what to see, everyone had come to the road with swollen faces and stood there. 'Why so late?' they asked, snatching the child from my hands.

'It's raining, mom. I couldn't get a bus properly. The child was also mischievous, didn't eat properly,' I said something or the other. 'The bus doesn't come only for you. It's the same for everyone. You should feed the children their fill. It's the rainy season, it's raining for the country, not just for you. If I see you not eating and not growing, it makes my stomach burn. How was it when you were here? A doll, a pumpkin flower. Is this how you raise children? Look at their eyes, look at their faces, 'If children eat, there is no harm, if it rains, there is no harm,' she started saying. 'What is this, mom, did you change the proverb?' I asked. 'The proverb should change, we should change, come, wash your hands and feet,' said mom.

Sixteen years have passed since my journey in memory of the Adri rain, which feels like yesterday. In my father's time, a palace. In my son's time, just a house. A mat on the floor. At this time, when the beard is eaten by a mouse and the child is chewing on a brush, where is the son, how is he, did he eat his fill? Shouldn't today's mothers say all this?
