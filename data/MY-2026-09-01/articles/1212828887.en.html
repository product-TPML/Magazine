The taste of a new crop

Jabihulla M. Asad

The simple answer to the question of why we read is: mental joy and development. Even those who read books for pastime are influenced by reading, whether they know it or not. It provides a healthy mindset necessary to understand life. Good works always provide the necessary windows to understand the present. Classic works, on the other hand, are timeless, taking the form of lamps that are relevant for all ages. With this understanding, 'Mayura' is constantly striving to include popular and public-friendly writings. As an example, you can look at the prose writings, especially the stories, in this issue. This issue contains stories by writers of various age groups. These stories are like creative responses to the anxieties and turmoil of our time. In addition to these, this issue contains two unpublished stories by Besagarahalli Ramanna, one of the best Kannada storytellers. These stories are haunting and bewildering to readers. They also serve as texts for new generation storytellers.

Although Kannada literature has seen excellent essayists, the essay genre has always been in a state of 'a bird here, a bud there'. Compared to stories and poems, experiments that attract attention in the essay genre are rare. This issue includes compositions by some essayists who are seriously involved in the essay genre. These compositions are noteworthy for their subtlety and emotional richness.

This is the time when the land should be covered with rain clouds, and the moisture of rain should fill the soil and the air. However, nature's calculations seem to be different. 'Mayura's' calculation is uniform; to offer readers the taste of a new crop of literature. 'Mayura' expects constructive notes on the taste of this issue.
