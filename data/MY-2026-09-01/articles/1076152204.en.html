‘There is grain, but no food
The world is on fire’
– D.R. Bendre

Just recently, a young man, hot-blooded and in his teens, who was enamored by the expensive, colorful, foreign-made mobile phone that just entered the market, was from a kingdom, like a crumbling relic, still immersed in the glory of the past, floating in a ruined pit, pumping, and constantly intoxicated by the oil in his stomach. For the fragmented pieces of an old relic of a kingdom that ruled about two and a half to three centuries ago, mobiles were the meditation. In the past, there were lazy people who could not enjoy the pleasure of kingship due to the company of women and liquor. This last and only offspring of such a royal family had two weaknesses. As already recorded, they were women and liquor. Now, as newly recorded, it is a colorful foreign-made mobile.

A devotional song, 'Brahma Satya, Jagat Mithya' (Brahma is truth, the world is illusion), written by Brahmin pundits for the royal family long ago, was being chanted. A boy, riding on a horse named Basanti, bought a seventeenth iPhone from a mobile shop in Mumbai, and was proceeding in a procession. At the same time, another boy, dancing and chanting 'Brahma Satya, Jagat Mithya', threw what some loudly called a toy bomb. This coincided with Basanti, who was near the boy chanting the name, and she got scared and bolted.

In the intoxication of oil, a ceasefire had to be declared just before surrendering to the chanting of 'Brahma Satya, Jagat Mithya'.

On the other side, for the drones of Iran and Iraq, the proverb 'Silence is golden, speech is charcoal' was cheered. The news that came in the procession was 'Oh phone, my call got cut and broken, my glasses fell into the gutter'. Hearing this, I found nothing surprising. The man who fell, crying 'I love you iPhone', to fulfill his desire, sat on the horse's back and reigned, like the last link in the royal family tree, fidgeting on his feet. The night before the first wedding anniversary, his wife, without telling or asking, ran away with a Hollywood actor. If we, like this 'prince', introspect every night, aren't we all devotees of some kind of horse, with his wife being a 'dream girl'?

Did they just say, 'Desire is a mirage horse...'? In short, all riders of the horse of desire crave 'this Basanti'. But the difference is, he has ridden a real-world horse, while many of us have ridden the mirage horse of the imaginary world. Another class consists of those who hang on to the tail of a racehorse and constantly swing in dreams. Say that there is no world without desire.

Recall Beechi's 'The Story of That Timma's Donkey', where a rider holds a handful of grass in front, and the donkey keeps running, hoping to get the grass. Don't you see the similarity between that donkey, which forgets the fatigue of running in its longing for grass, and this human animal, who is chasing the dream of a mobile phone! You can call me a donkey, but I am not ready to get off the back of this mirage horse of desire. For me, who has come to earth to savor the essence of family life, such an ascetic life is certainly not possible.

Look at those who wear the face of detachment; they say that desire is the root of sorrow, and they sour a happy life. They are petty pleasure-seekers who sing 'Our home is there, here it is just for show' and flare their nostrils at the stench of 'desire'.

Instead of sweating like a camel in the desert, what fun can be found in dreaming with such eyes, like a fish floating in the pond of longing, overflowing with desires? Some might laugh and say, 'A cat that cannot jump to the roof desires to climb to the sky.' They might say that for one who cannot dance, the ground is crooked. Let it be.

Like the character Micawber in Charles Dickens's novel 'David Copperfield', only these riders of the mirage horse have such a romantic mind that sees sweet dreams everywhere. This mirage horse is the foundation of all our castles in the air.

How many times must I have thought, 'Shouldn't we, at least by looking at the sky-bird that yearns for the setting of the sun, the lord of the horizons, unfurl our parachute of desire?' Those who skillfully part their hair on a bald head always inspire me. Even if one sleeps in a dilapidated धर्मशाला, wasn't the image of the palace's inner chambers hidden in the mind of the beggar! For a moment, the palace was his own property.

Our dreams are more beautiful than our lives. The thrill of hunting is not there when the prey is caught. Similarly, we start dreaming now for some future good fortune.

For how long have athletes not ridden the mirage horse of desire for the gold and silver medals that will adorn their necks one day! Similarly, our politicians, artists, great poets, and all of us. Recall the lines of the movie song, 'Desire is ahead, humans are behind it'.

This is a kind of longing, and those who bear the burden of this longing are not easily disturbed. They are the ones who find thrill in turning a stick into a hill, and that hill into a mountain. Even if they are exhausted in this adventure, they do not give up their boasting; even if they are old, they do not give up the struggle. They are fortunate souls like our poet Bendre Ajja, who gave wings to their longing, adorned themselves with a garland of stars, and made the sun and moon their eyes! Better than the fox who complains that the grapes are sour the further they fly, are the excellent Nalas who mix the seasoning of a feast into the stale rice of dreams and eat it as a delicacy.

You know the story of a sanyasi who, disgusted with life, became a monk, but then grew his family's tail with the need for a cat, a cow, and a woman. Is there a shortage of arrows of desire in the quiver of our lives? Despite all this, just because some philosopher said that this life is just a three-day fair, and why all this worry, we cannot be free from worry. If we decide to let go and put all our family worries aside and live carefree, people in this world will label us 'the bull of the fair'. Even if we let go of worry, it must let go of us. For someone who says they have no worries, that itself becomes a big worry.

That day, my colleague Vitthal came to my house with his head in his hands. The beloved, whom he thought loved him more than life itself, was about to marry someone else. This had become a thorn in Vitthal's life, tormenting him.

‘Why are you so worried, you fool?’ I tried to console him with various words. No, Vitthal's sour face did not brighten. Then, I unveiled my philosophy of longing.

‘Look, Vittu, the joy of loving is not in marrying that lover. Aren't many people who had love marriages now cutting ropes in front of your eyes?’ At these words, Vittu's face brightened a little.

‘But still, this pain I'm feeling now...’ He wiped away two teardrops from the corner of his eye with his left hand, poor fellow.

‘Oh, you fool! Why do you fall into the pit by keeping the limestone of old memories in your lap? Try sailing a boat in the pond of new dreams, you'll become wise.’ These comforting words must have appealed to Vitthal, his face shone with serenity.

My recitation of longing continued. 'Your desire to marry the one you loved and live happily might have been buried, but its longing remains alive. This will not perish as long as you live, nor will it become old. Experience in longing what is impossible in your life. For this, there will be no disputes from anyone. And, you don't know what longing is, it's riding the mirage horse,' I said, laughing.

‘I know, I know,’ Vittu nodded. With a wide smile, he said, ‘If you eat mandigi in your mind, you don't have to pay for wheat, ha ha...’ He laughed and headed home. My theory had finally clicked.

Oh... Basanti
You have no worries
Saturday market

Now, the same Vittu is married to another girl and is a father of three children. He often forgets the occasional annoyance from his wife by remembering his former beloved. What if his beloved didn't become his wife? She lives in his mind, doesn't she...! There's no war between co-wives. No mutual grinding of teeth in jealousy. It's like secretly hiding black money.
However, not everyone who rides the mirage horse of desire might have prospered. They might not have gotten the 'I love you iPhone-10'. Still, where can everyone find a life as entertaining as that of the mirage horse riders? Even if we assume that some will eventually drown in disappointment, they would have enjoyed a fun swim in the pond of longing for some time. They are the ones who say that happiness is enough. Aren't we all dreamers day and night? As poet Nisar says, 'Those who dream of wealth in stone, springs in the ground, and paths in the forest'.

Yes, one doesn't have to become a Siddhartha who leaves the palace of happiness and goes to the forest to become a Buddha. Our life is a game of chess with a million paths. What wisdom is there in doing Dravidian pranayama, getting caught in many anxieties, and turning life into dust?

Don't worry, let desires keep coming in rows. Longing will always be there as a wink of what is to come.

Oh, sir, tell me, who has desire spared? The kings and emperors of this world are certainly no exception. Even renunciates have become pleasure-seekers. A yogi has no caste, the ocean has no fixed abode. Our yogis too are enchanted by the mirage horse. Even a Vishwamitra like Vishwamitra danced 'Tha... Thai... Thaka Thai...' for Menaka. And what a longing the gods and goddesses of Devaloka have! The game played by Lord Indra for his desire for Anasuya Devi. Dinakara descended to earth for Kunti! It would take reams of paper if one were to describe such 'leelayana'.

If Mother Sita had not desired the illusory deer, Lakshman would not have crossed the line, her abduction by Ravana, and the subsequent war between Rama and Ravana, where would that Ramayana have taken place? Poet Valmiki would not have had the fortune to compose an epic. Similarly, one can say that Dharmaraja's desire to win in gambling, which haunted him like a mirage horse, was for the good. Wasn't it for that reason that the Pandavas lost Draupadi, her disrobing, the Kurukshetra war? The teaching of the Gita by Lord Krishna... do you agree that the mirage horse of desire is the root of all these!
Similarly, chasing the mirage horse of lottery, we buy tickets, not one or two, but five or ten. Then, until the draw, we become heroes of longing, floating and floating in the world of dreams. We show the same ticket to our sulking wife, adorn her hair with celestial jasmine, and appease her! With the money borrowed from her, we keep buying lotteries again and again. There is a horse, and there is a field. We are indeed brave and courageous heroes who know how to ride the given horse. Like Hema Malini's 'Basanti' in 'Sholay', our mirage horse is always ever-ready.

Chickpeas, horse gram, fodder – a fun horse that will never 'dimsandis' you. Once you mount it, like Shishunala Sharif's fun horse, it will take you to the upper street. So why delay? Get ready for the ride. Mount the mirage horse of Basanti (progeny) and forget the worries of life. iPhone – eleven, twelve, seventeen,
'Ha...' seventeen... my love darlings
Are said to be storming the market again.

Chal Chal Chal... Re Basanti!
