There is a certain variety and attraction in the snacks, meals and food of the coastal region of Karnataka. 'Huriyakki Unde' is one such rare delicacy. The coastal delicacy 'Huriyakki Unde' is so delicious that once you eat it, you won't want to stop. By the way, Huriyakki Unde is mostly prepared during the rainy season. The reason is that it controls cold and provides warmth to the body.

Making Huriyakki Unde is not an easy task. The 'Pandya' paddy grown in the paddy fields is traditionally pounded to make Kuchchakki (parboiled rice), which is then washed and dried in the sun on a thin cloth. The dried rice should be roasted evenly in an earthen pot over a wood fire using a wooden ladle. After grinding the roasted rice into flour, one should not wait long. In a pot, thin jaggery and dried coconut (not copra) gratings should be mixed and cooked evenly. Once it reaches the right consistency, mix the flour well with it, apply coconut oil to your hands and make the Huriyakki Unde.

Only experienced Huriyakki Unde makers should be able to achieve this consistency. If the consistency of coconut gratings and jaggery is wrong, the Huriyakki Unde will be as hard as iron and inedible. If thin jaggery from Hiregutthi, Kumta taluk, Uttara Kannada district is used, the Huriyakki Unde will be more delicious and can be stored for many days.

This Huriyakki Unde is available in only a few small shops in taluks like Karwar, Ankola, Kumta, Honnavar, Bhatkal.

In these modern days, instead of wood fire, Pandya paddy Kuchchakki, flour made from grinding stone, earthen pots, and earthen pans, gas stoves, mixers, and steel utensils are being used. For taste, groundnuts, raisins, cashews, etc., can be seen mixed in Huriyakki Unde. However, in many villages on the coast of Uttara Kannada district, Huriyakki Unde is still traditionally prepared.g
