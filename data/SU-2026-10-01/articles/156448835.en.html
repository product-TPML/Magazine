I hate you. This is my last letter.

A pink balloon floated towards the girl who was sitting with her feet in the river water, having gone for an evening stroll near the river flowing behind her house. The balloon hit her foot and a nearby stone, swaying there for a while. Nature always brings certain things to our attention that we must notice. Perhaps that's why the paper inside the balloon caught her eye. Suspecting that it was an unusual act for a paper to go inside a balloon that was inflated and tied at the end with a string, the girl picked up the balloon. Some letters on the paper inside were vaguely visible. With curiosity, she untied the string of the balloon. She took out the paper inside. That's when she saw these lines. The entire paper contained only these two sentences. 'I hate you. This is my last letter'.

Not two, but three. At the bottom left of the letter, it simply said 'Yours, the hater'. Since it wasn't the subject matter of the letter, one might assume there were only two sentences in the letter. The girl smiled when she saw these lines. She looked around. The river flowed on its own course. Thinking it was better not to get involved in such matters, she decided to put the letter back in the balloon as it was and let it go. But after she picked up the paper, she didn't care about the balloon, and it had continued its journey with the river. Perhaps it knew its destination? Or perhaps it understood the reality that a messenger's job is only to deliver the message, not to control it, and so it disappeared. The girl, holding the paper in her hand and lost in thought, left the three-sentence letter there and started to leave. After walking fifty steps, she felt she had made a mistake. She turned back, made sure no one was around, picked up the letter, gently folded it, and held it in her fist. She hurried home and placed the letter under her bed. She was aware of the privacy she had in her village home. She knew that no one would come under her bed to search for this letter.

When she went to bed that night, she took out the letter and looked at it again. The line 'Yours, the hater' seemed very strange. When there are quarrels in love, saying 'I hate you' is common. But declaring hatred like this seemed a bit contradictory. She thought there was something special about the person who said that. And it also said 'This is my last letter', so there must have been many letters exchanged until now, and she thought it must have been written to someone who didn't respond properly. Whatever it was, she decided not to get involved in this entanglement and resolved to go to the river the next day, put the letter back in the pink balloon, and let it go in the river.

***

As decided, when she came to the river the next evening, the balloon was present as usual. Like a child secretly taking sweets from a box when its mother isn't looking, she picked up the letter. She let the balloon go its way.

'I hate you. This is not my last letter. I will not let the injustice done to me happen to anyone else,' was the message in it. Reading this made her a little uneasy. If something that started as a whim goes to the extent of giving a warning, it's natural for anyone to be scared. That whole night, many thoughts came to her. Could this letter have been addressed to her? If so, whom had she wronged? In this confusion, the girl couldn't sleep all night. On one hand, this might not be related to her at all. Why should she bother so much? Some girl might have cheated on some boy in love. That's why he might be behaving so excessively. Thinking that she didn't need to worry so much about this, she decided not to go to the river that evening and got busy with her daily chores. She didn't go to the river that day. But that night, another thought came to her; if she didn't even know whether this letter was addressed to her or not, why should she be scared? Thinking this, when she came to the river the next day, two letters were present. She hurriedly took them and read them.

'I hate you. I have made my final decision. The day a rain destroyed my life, you were dancing in the rain, getting wet. You, who should have put your hand on my shoulder, remained silent like someone with a broken hand. That's when I had to make a difficult decision.'

- Yours, the hater...

This letter felt like a personal accusation and attack on her. Yet, she took out the other letter that was with it. It was written:

'I hate you. Thousands of hands long to embrace a child. But how can one forgive a treacherous mind that slips away from a child's hand?'

-Yours, the hater

Seeing the inconsistent topics being brought up from letter to letter, her initial indifference was replaced by a special interest. She kept all the letters. After this, the sixth, seventh, eighth, and ninth letters were very brief and subtle.

'He took the poison. I am the one who is poisoned every day...'

'You didn't utter a word... you could have at least put a lamp on the cross wall.'

'I get hungry, for that reason, even if it feels like a snake is crawling on my stomach, I forcibly hug my shoulders... Do I get happiness? Do I give it? Do I get and lose it? I still don't understand...'

'You were there when the hands that taught me to walk were cut off by those very hands... but?'

***

Thus, in the letters that gradually came, the line 'Yours, the hater' disappeared. Rather than pinpointing from which letter it was absent, perhaps her growing interest brought about such a change in the lines of these letters. Only the line in the tenth letter, 'Every letter deserves an answer. That is the right of the letter writer,' had shaken her. It had instilled fear in her. But that fear did not prevent her from coming to the river. Instead, it increased her desire to unravel the mystery of those letters. She gathered all ten letters and compared the subject matter in all of them. No matter how she analyzed it, she couldn't come to any conclusion about whether the writer was a man or a woman, known or unknown. Only one thing was confirmed: new letters were coming because they were continuously arriving and reaching the correct address. That is, she realized they were addressed to her. She decided she wouldn't rest until she found out who was doing this. She examined all the letters again. No clue was found. But a mystery never knows it is a mystery. That's why it often reveals itself naturally.

***

When some more letters, carrying similar mysteries, floated down the river, the girl became even more confused. Some lines in them increased her determination to find the origin of the letters.

'How did the villagers not know anything about what happened to me in the school toilet? I need to know.'

'They beat me like a cow outside the temple.'

'On the housewarming day of the new house across the valley, I was waiting for the Mysore Pak served on the banana leaf. You didn't even look at me.'

'The boy who went to graze cattle on the lake bund was startled to see the body of Patel's eldest son floating, I was with him that day.'

'What happened when a teacher from the village was found disheveled with Eeravva from the street near the field fence, and a panchayat was called?'

***

Thus, day by day, the mystery of the letters disappeared. As ignorance disappears, mystery gains clarity. Now, she was no longer curious about who might be writing the letters. Instead, only the interest in what event might be written about in the next letter remained. However, determined to find out who was writing these things so accurately, she traveled through all the villages upstream from her village along the river. There was no specific questionnaire to find those behind such strange letters! She returned without finding any clues in any of the villages. That evening, when she went to the river, the letter that came said:

'Your story is like a thief who came secretly, sitting on the police station steps, waiting to complain about the owner.'

These lines immediately angered her, but they also pushed her into some inexpressible distress. She looked at the river very affectionately. The waves appearing here and there in the calmly flowing river seemed to mock her. She felt humiliated. This river behind her house had never behaved like this in all these years. As she started towards home, annoyed with the river, she felt as if someone had pulled her hand. Just like the first day the letter came, she had turned back after fifty steps, the same happened today.

'By the time you come here tomorrow evening, you might know who is behind this. Don't worry, think,' the river seemed to say.

She reached home, grappling with this dilemma. That night, it occurred to her that there must be some special reason behind the disappearance of the 'I hate you' line in the initial letters. And she also understood that the content of the later letters was scattered. Although she wished this would be resolved by tomorrow evening, as the river had said, she couldn't figure out the way.

The next day, she was walking down the village street. As she passed each house, it became clear that the contents of each letter she had received resembled someone, somewhere. More light dawned when the maid who had been sexually harassed in the school toilet stood in front of her.

The one who is poisoned every day...

Those who cut off the hands that led them...

The one who moved away from life on the day it rained...

Someone like Eeravva who stood on the panchayat platform...

The hunger waiting for Mysore Pak outside the compound...

And many others...

Thus, she met the characters of each letter. It became clear to her how it was possible that she herself was the one who came to the police station to complain about the owner with stolen goods...

***

The next evening, she came to the river a little earlier than usual and sat down. She enjoyed a fish spa with the biting fish in the river.

No letter came...

She waited...

A balloon was floating in the distance...

There was no letter inside it...

She opened the balloon...

She pretended to take the letter, tied the balloon again, and let it float away...

The fish gently bit her feet, making her feel good, as if healing something...

As this therapy ended, she looked at the river intently before heading home. Many balloons filled her sight.

When she went to bed that night, she read the letter that had (not) come that day.

'Every letter finds its address.

Yours, the one who cannot hate...'

Reading only this, she carefully kept it with the other letters she had. In the ambiguity of whether those letters were written for her or if she had written them herself, the path to clarity began to appear. g
