Veena Shankar

I was born and raised in Banavara, Arasikere, the land of coconuts. Since my husband had a job in Harihara, near Davanagere, I came to Harihara. The new place, its heat, humidity, and the rough language of the people, all felt new in a way. The language and cooking of these people are all new. Even though their language is a bit rough, they have good hearts. Davanagere and Harihara were like twin cities. Since I lived there for thirty-five years, and my children's schooling and studies went well, we became like people from that region. Just like "Bangalore Kannada, Mangalore Kannada, Dharwad Kannada are all one," we also got used to that language. Now, when people from Bangalore hear us speak, they immediately ask, "Are you from North Karnataka?"

Harihara was in Dharwad district. Davanagere was part of Chitradurga district. In 1992, Chief Minister J.H. Patel made Davanagere an independent district, including Davanagere, Harihara, Jagalur, Honnali, Channagiri, and Nyamathi. Davanagere is a major district located in the central part of Karnataka. At one time, it was famous for its cotton and textile industry, earning the name 'Manchester of Karnataka'. There were major cotton textile mills, but now they are all closed. However, Davanagere's large textile shop, B.S. Chennabasappa Sons, is a big name in Karnataka. Many people come from all parts of the state for cloth trade.

Historically known as 'Davanagiri', Davanagere was a jagir for Maratha leaders during Tipu Sultan's reign. Later, it grew into a trade center, as the elders say.

Moreover, if you go to Bangalore, our state capital, the Kannada spoken there is influenced by foreign languages, not just our country's languages. We, the people of Davanagere, haven't given such familiarity to others. Drinking the pure water of the Tungabhadra river, eating Benne Dose, Nargis Mandakki, and Mirchi, they speak with the same taste. Davanagere chilies are very spicy, but when you eat Benne Dose chutney and Nargis Mandakki, their taste is very appealing to the mind. The same goes for Davanagere language.

Business is booming in Davanagere. Here, crops like cotton, maize, groundnut, sunflower, jowar, paddy, and many others are grown, and they are also brought from other districts of Karnataka for trade.

Vidyasaraswati resides in Davanagere. There are colleges here with departments of medicine, engineering, fine arts, fashion design, arts, commerce, and education.

Since there are large hospitals and renowned doctors here, people from all parts of our district come here to take care of their health.

Selected for the Smart City project, there is also a glass house here, similar to Bangalore. This shows the pride of our town. The Kunduvada lake on the outskirts of Davanagere is good for morning walks.

Bringing stones from Duggatti village, a temple was built and Durgadevi was consecrated. The Durgambha temple is very beautiful to behold. On Tuesdays and Fridays, the women of our town go to the temple and perform puja. A big fair is held once every two years.

As soon as Davanagere is mentioned, no matter which town or people you ask, the first thing they say is the name Benne Dose. That's true. Along with it, the aroma of Nargis Mandakki and Mirchi Bajji fills the town. The crispy dose made on a wood stove, with potato curry, spicy thick chutney, and plenty of fresh butter, makes you want to keep eating. Then, you should eat Nargis Mandakki made with finely chopped onions, tasty masala, and roasted gram powder, along with Mirchi Bajji, and then drink a glass of masala tea.

In addition to this, the vermicelli made in cottage industries has a history of fifty years. It doesn't spoil even if kept for a year. On festive days, when vermicelli is strained, the vermicelli payasam, shining like silk, not sticking together, smooth and sliding down the throat, has a different charm. When relatives visit, the elders lovingly say, "I'll strain vermicelli, eat and go," and these words bring as much joy as eating payasam. Besides, Jowar Rotti, Ennegayi, lentil curry, Gulladki Undi, Paddu, and various types of Holige are also special foods of Davanagere.

Next is our Harihara. It is called 'Dakshina Kashi'. Here is the confluence of the Tunga-Bhadra rivers, and on the banks of this river is the Raghavendra Math. It is also called Chikka Mantralaya. The Harihareshwara temple from the Hoysala period is very beautiful. It is a stone temple, and the skillful carvings of the sculptor can be seen all around the temple. The artistic ninety pillars in the main hall are indescribable. Once a year, the Harihareshwara Rathotsava is celebrated with grandeur.

Furthermore, our district's Kondajji lake is located in a dense forest area. The beautiful lake, built by constructing a dam between two hills, is very charming. Birds migrate here, and scout and guide training also takes place here. Shanti Sagara (Sulekere) in Channagiri taluk has Asia's largest man-made lake. Many people come here for its beautiful natural environment, boating, and bird watching.

The Kalleshwara Swamy temple in Bagali, built during the Kalyana Chalukya period, is very good. Here, ancient inscriptions, hero stones, and finely carved stone idols are very beautiful. The Bhimeshwara temple in Nilagunda, built during the Kalyana Chalukya period, is also there. Between Harihara and Davanagere is the Bathi hill. On it are the Dargah of Chaman Shahawari and the Dhanvantari sacred forest.

Helavanakatte Giriyamma, who used to sing 'Honnu Ta Gubbi Honnu Ta, Namma Chinmaya Mooruti Cheluva Rangana Kaige', was from Komaranahalli in our Davanagere district. The elders say that she was a great devotee of Ranganatha and lived in the Komaranahalli Ranganathaswamy temple, composing famous kirtanas. There is also a Datta Mantapa in the premises of this ashram.

The largest Pushkarini in Karnataka, built in the sixteenth century by Palegar Hiriya Hanumappa Nayaka, called 'Hondaa', is in Santebennur in our district. Many people come to see it, and you don't have to pay to see it.

The Kannada spoken in our Davanagere is not soft. Our people's language may sound rough, but their hearts are very good. Political people have also made a big name. Don't worry about food. There are big hotels. Benne Dose, Mirchi Mandakki are very cheap. There are many gold and silver shops, and clothing stores. Also, the train and bus facilities for coming here are good.
