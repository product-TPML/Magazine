Dog bites and their care can disrupt many people's daily work. Global Problem

Rabies is a viral disease found in mammals, including humans, domestic animals (dogs, cats, cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, etc.), and wild animals (foxes, wolves, deer, etc.). The Lyssavirus is the causative agent of rabies. The virus is present in high concentrations in the brain and saliva of infected animals. A rabid dog exhibits behavior that doesn't even recognize its beloved owner. It can drool excessively and attack humans or other animals without any provocation. The virus enters the body through a dog bite. It penetrates and damages the central nervous system. Symptoms can appear within a few weeks to several months after being bitten by a rabid dog. Once symptoms appear, death is certain within a few days. If bitten by any animal infected with rabies, immediate vaccination can prevent rabies. This is the only way to escape rabies.

According to the World Health Organization report, rabies claims 59,000 lives worldwide every year. More than 95% of these deaths occur in economically backward countries in Asia and Africa. India alone accounts for 33-35% of human rabies cases. It should be noted from these statistics that India is also the country with the highest number of stray dogs in the world. The remaining handful of rabies cases are caused by cats or wild animals. 80% of rabies cases in India are found in rural areas, and children account for 40% of those affected by the disease.

Recognizing its seriousness, global organizations such as the World Health Organization, the World Organisation for Animal Health, the Food and Agriculture Organization, and the Global Alliance for Rabies Control implemented the 'Zero by Thirty' program worldwide in 2018. This is an ambitious project aiming for no human deaths from rabies infection by the end of 2030. After COVID, India also launched a National Action Plan for Rabies Elimination in September 2021, working in line with the global aspiration. The project has now entered the 'Mop Up' phase for 2026-2030. During this period, many incidents of dog bites and rabies infection have come to the forefront in our country, and the Supreme Court has directed states to relocate stray dogs from residential areas and provide alternative arrangements.

How to prevent it?
Rabies is a preventable disease. To prevent humans from contracting rabies, the disease must primarily be controlled in dogs. We have ready technical information to prevent rabies in dogs 100%. Regular vaccination of dogs against rabies and controlling the number of stray dogs through sterilization surgery are effective measures. The relationship between humans and dogs is emotional. Owners vaccinate their pet dogs against rabies. However, citizens who feed and care for stray dogs, despite knowing the need for rabies vaccination for them, do not take responsibility. In such situations, volunteers should join hands with local organizations. Society must accept and embrace the responsibility of sterilizing stray dogs under the animal birth control program and collectively vaccinating them against rabies.

What to do when bitten by a dog?

To prevent rabies infection from a dog bite, the World Health Organization's PEP (Post Exposure Prophylaxis) recommendations are followed. Accordingly, no dog bite should be ignored. The first step is to thoroughly wash the wound with soap and clean water for 15 minutes. Do not stitch the wound; do not bandage it. Immediately visit the nearest primary health center and receive the series of rabies vaccinations given for dog bites and, in cases of severe wounds, the rabies immunoglobulin treatment. PEP is provided free of charge under the National Rabies Control Program.

World Rabies Day

September 28 is the day Louis Pasteur, the great humanitarian and scientist who discovered the rabies vaccine, passed away. In his memory, many organizations worldwide undertake work to raise public awareness about rabies on that day. Many programs are held, such as vaccinating dogs and raising awareness about the disease through seminars.
