Air Show

Nemichandra

On September 1, 2026, Indian Air Force officer Shakti Sharma was appointed 'Air Vice Marshal'. It is special that she is the first woman officer from the non-medical field to attain this rank. 'Air Vice Marshal' is a two-star rank, equivalent to 'Major General' in the Army and 'Rear Admiral' in the Navy.

Today, the Chief of the Air Staff, Air Chief Marshal Amar Preet Singh, holds a four-star rank. The two stars shining on the collar of Shakti Sharma's blue uniform today are a beacon of hope that women officers may one day have the opportunity to adorn that position. Until now, women in the non-medical field of the three services had not been appointed to a two-star rank. Therefore, this rank awarded to Shakti Sharma is a milestone in the history of the armed forces.

In 2019, I had the opportunity to meet Shakti Sharma. Although I had participated in every air show of Aero India since its inception in 1996 due to my profession in an aircraft factory, the Aero India 2019 international air show was an unforgettable experience in my life. On February 23, 2019, a video film titled 'Queens of the Sky' and a postage stamp were released, recognizing the contribution of women to the aviation sector at Aero India 2019. That day was celebrated as Women in Aviation Day.

On that day, besides Shakti Sharma, it was a matter of pride to see many young women officers shining in military uniforms, and I enthusiastically took their photos. At that time, Shakti Sharma was introduced to us as the first woman Group Captain of the Air Force. Later, after rising to the rank of Air Commodore, she has now been given the position of Assistant Chief of Air Staff (Education). Shakti Sharma, who joined the education and training division of the Indian Air Force on June 18, 1994, has excelled in various roles during this long period of 32 years; she has served in training units, field stations, command headquarters, and 'Vayu Bhawan', the Air Force Headquarters in New Delhi.

She played a key role in establishing Indian Air Force research 'study chairs' at IIT Kanpur and Pune University. She is the first woman officer of the three services to serve as the Principal of the 'Sainik School' in Kapurthala, Punjab.

Women have been in the military as medical officers for a long time. As nurses, Indian women joined the military medical corps even before independence. In 1958, women doctors were given the opportunity to work as full-time officers in the Army Medical Corps, on par with men.

Padma Bandopadhyay was the first woman in the Air Force to attain the rank of 'Air Marshal' in 2002. Before her in the Indian Army, Surgeon Vice Admiral Punita Arora was a medical officer who had attained a 'three-star' rank. She joined the army in 1968 and was a 'Flag Officer' in both the Indian Navy and the Army. However, the entry of women into the military in non-medical fields was not easy.

In 1981, when I was in my final year of engineering, naval officers visited our college. Two of us girls went and sat with my male classmates. The naval officer, seeing us, informed us that 'women are not allowed in the army' and sent us out. It was then that I realized that in the land of Kittur Rani Chennamma, Jhansi Rani Lakshmibai, Rani Abbakka, and Razia Sultana, women were not allowed into the army.

Entry into the Air Force

In 1992, for the first time, the Indian Air Force opened its doors to women on its 60th birthday. It invited women to join the Air Force with the slogan 'Giving Wings to Your Dreams'. Women were selected by competing with men at an all-India level. On September 2, 1994, newspapers announced 'Women pilots team soon for Air Force'. Bengaluru newspapers enthusiastically published that 'seven young women are on the threshold of making history in the nation's military aviation'; the reason being that the training of these girls was taking place in Bengaluru.

On that day, Flight Cadet Harita Kaur Deol from Chandigarh became the first woman pilot in the Air Force to fly an aircraft solo when she flew the Air Force's 'Avro' - a large transport aircraft - at Yelahanka Air Force Station, Bengaluru. Immediately after her, her classmates, Flight Cadet Archana Kapoor from Delhi and Flight Cadet Bindu Sebastian from Kerala, flew Avro aircraft solo on the same day.

The other four young women in the first batch were Priya Nalagundwar from Bhopal, Pamela Rodrigues from Mumbai, Anita Sinha from Delhi, and Priya Pal from Kerala. I had met Priya Pal among the women of this first batch. It was then that I realized that not all these girls came from military families; many of them joined the Air Force with a pure desire to fly.

There were two other women pilots in this first batch. They were helicopter pilots Cheryl Dutta and Simran Sodhi. Cheryl Dutta became the first woman to fly a helicopter in the Air Force. Finally, women were allowed into the Air Force, a male-dominated field. In the same year, the Army and Navy also opened their doors to women.

Srividya Rajan and Gunjan Saxena, famously known as 'Kargil Girl', who entered the Air Force in the fourth batch, were deployed to the battlefield. When they were given wings (indicating they were pilots on their shoulders) at the passing out parade after graduating from the academy in June 1996, they truly felt the joy of having two wings to fly.

Gunjan and Srividya were posted to Udhampur as the first women officers. Naturally, these two women had to face all the initial obstacles. Initially, there were no women's toilets. There were no separate arrangements for women to change clothes. They also had accommodation in the same mess. But no one behaved indecently. In my career, I had faced the same problem when I went to military bases and firing ranges for test flights of military helicopters. But my problem was for a few days and weeks. Theirs was not like that; these brave women, who did not count the inconveniences, focused on their flight training.

In May 1999, the Kargil War began. There were casualties on the battlefield; the need for skilled pilots increased. At such a time, these women who had joined the army got entry into the battlefield. Flying helicopters at an altitude of 13,000 to 18,000 feet, amidst a hail of bullets on the battlefield, and landing helicopters anywhere without a proper place required a strong heart.

Within a few days, both Srividya and Gunjan had flown in Kargil. For 25 consecutive days, Gunjan flew three or four sorties a day, safely transporting hundreds of injured. Gunjan was part of 'Operation Vijay' until the end of the war. But Gunjan Saxena, who bravely excelled in the path of conflict, had to leave the Air Force in 2004 after her 'Short Service Commission' period ended, simply because she was a woman and did not get a 'Permanent Commission'.

Historic Verdict

Although the Indian Armed Forces opened their doors to women in 1992, they prevented them from rising in rank and reaching commander positions. Women filed writ petitions in the Delhi High Court in 2003 seeking equal opportunities in the military. On February 17, 2020, the Supreme Court delivered a landmark judgment removing the limitation on opportunities for women officers in the Indian Army. The government, which had argued that 'women cannot be sent to conflict zones without minimum facilities', admitted before the court that 30% of women had been deputed even to such 'conflict zones'. This historic judgment of the Supreme Court, which stated that women should also have opportunities for leadership positions in command roles, allowed women to rise in the military.

Moreover, even though women had been flying transport aircraft and helicopters in the Air Force for two and a half decades, they did not have the opportunity to fly fighter aircraft. In October 2015, women were allowed to fly supersonic fighter aircraft. In 2016, Flying Officers Avani Chaturvedi, Bhawana Kanth, and Mohana Singh became India's first women jet fighter pilots. Among them, Squadron Leader Bhawana Kanth became the first woman officer of the Air Force to qualify as a 'Flight Combat Leader' and receive the prestigious 'Top Gun' distinction last month. She received extremely rigorous air combat training at the 'Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment' in Gwalior. This Air Force training institution trains only one percent of the best fighter pilots. Bhawana has now qualified for leadership in advanced air combat operations and tactical missions.

In 2023, on the occasion of International Women's Day, the Indian Air Force appointed Group Captain Shaliza Dhami as the first woman commander of its combat unit. This was an opportunity to command a 'Missile Squadron' facing Pakistan. She had joined the Air Force as a helicopter pilot in 2003. She had 2800 hours of flying experience. Earlier, Shaliza had also served as 'Flight Commander' of a helicopter unit in the western sector. She had experience leading the force as 'Air Officer Commanding in Chief' on two occasions.

In the same year, January 2023, Group Captain Shiv Chauhan was selected as the first woman officer to the 'Kumar' post near the Siachen Glacier, the world's highest battlefield. This area in the Siachen Glacier, which is a conflict zone between India and Pakistan, is located at an altitude of 15,632 feet above sea level. Flying at this altitude is extremely difficult; Shiv Chauhan had earned such an opportunity by demonstrating her immense confidence, strength, and ability during training.

In 'Operation Sindoor' conducted in May 2025, in response to last year's Pahalgam terrorist attack targeting Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, many women pilots of the army participated as 'Combat Pilots' and 'Weapon System Operators' managing weapons. This team also included a woman pilot who flew a 'Sukhoi' fighter jet. They destroyed terrorist bases by targeting advanced missiles like 'Rampage' and 'BrahMos'. Her name has not been disclosed yet.

Air Vice Marshal Shakti Sharma, standing today at the pinnacle of all these achievements, has inspired thousands of brave women; she has brought pride to women in participating in national defense.
