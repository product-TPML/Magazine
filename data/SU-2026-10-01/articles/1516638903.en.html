Waking up in the morning, household chores, children's studies, office, cooking, relatives, health, finances... thus, one task or another throughout the day. By the time everything is finished, it's night. The question "When do I live for myself?" might have arisen in the minds of many women.

When middle age arrives, another worry. The question "How to make life beautiful from now on?" haunts them.

Some simple techniques from Japanese life can help with this. In Japan, instead of changing life with big changes, more importance is given to improving it through small daily changes. The ten concepts in their lives are very useful for our lives too.

1. Kaizen – A little improvement every day.
'Kaizen' means continuous improvement. When we want change in life, we make big decisions. We decide, 'From now on, I will exercise for an hour every day', 'I will completely give up sweets', 'I will read for two hours every day'. After a few days, that enthusiasm diminishes. Kaizen says something different. Instead of a big change, take small steps. Walk for ten minutes every day. Read one page. Remove one unnecessary item from the kitchen. Save a little money every month. If such small changes continue, a big result will be achieved by the weekend. 'A little better today than yesterday' – this is the essence of Kaizen.

2. Pomodoro – Time limit for work.
There is no end to household chores. If you keep thinking about doing this and that, your mind gets tired.
The 'Pomodoro technique' is helpful for this. Usually, a method of 25 minutes of work, 5 minutes of break is used. For example, if there is a task of arranging clothes, focus on it for 25 minutes. Then sit for five minutes. You can relax by drinking a cup of tea without looking at your mobile. Instead of thinking about ten tasks at once, doing one task at a time reduces stress.

3. Ikigai – A reason to wake up in the morning.
The word 'Ikigai' is seen as a key concept in Japanese philosophy of life. Simply put, it is finding our own answer to the question, 'Why am I living? What is the meaning of my life?' This doesn't have to be a great achievement. For a mother, raising children can be her Ikigai. For another, it might be gardening. For someone else, music, reading, social service, or spending time with grandchildren. Don't think 'my work is over' after retirement. Your life still has meaning. Do one thing every day that brings you joy.

4. Shinrin-yoku – Walk with nature.
'Shinrin-yoku' means spending time peacefully in a forest atmosphere, i.e., 'Forest Bathing'.

You don't have to go to a forest for this. You can walk among the trees in a nearby park. You can sit in your rooftop garden. In the morning, you can put your mobile aside for a while, listening to the sound of birds. Our lives are trapped between screens. Even if for a short time each day, connecting with nature gives peace to the mind.

5. Osouji – Cleanse not just the house, but also the mind.
In Japan, 'Osouji' is used in the sense of a large-scale cleaning operation. Our homes are filled with many things that have not been used for many years. We keep them thinking 'they might be useful one day'. Once, look at your house and ask: 'Is this really necessary?' As unnecessary items are reduced, space is created in the house. The mind also feels lighter. This technique can also be applied to relationships. How long should we carry old anger, sadness, and dissatisfaction? While cleaning the house, let's also clean the mind.

6. Wabi-sabi – Don't chase perfection.
We have a strong desire for everything to be perfect. The house should always be neat, children should always behave well, we should always look beautiful... But life is not like that. The Japanese aesthetic perspective of 'Wabi-sabi' reminds us that there is beauty even in imperfection. The lines of age on the face, memories of an old house, a slightly crooked clay cup – all these are marks of life. Aging is not a defect; it is proof that we are alive.

7. Omotenashi – Warmth in the work you do.
'Omotenashi' is the Japanese culture of treating guests with warmth. There should be care even in the small things we do.

Let there be warmth even when offering water to a guest who comes to our house. When husband and wife talk to each other, let the mobile be put aside. When listening to a child, talk eye to eye. Love is not always a big gift; it is more visible in small acts of care.

8. Gambaru – Don't give up when difficulties arise.
'Gambaru' is the mindset of continuing to try even in the face of difficulties. Not everything in life goes our way. Children's problems, financial difficulties, job stress, relationship crises – one challenge or another arises. At such times, instead of giving up saying 'I can't do it', tell yourself 'I will do what is possible today'. One step... another step... continuing like this is the path to victory.

9. Chado – The art of living slowly.
The Japanese tea culture 'Chado' teaches us a beautiful lesson. To fully experience a moment.
We look at our mobile while eating. We think about the next task even while drinking tea. Our mind is elsewhere when talking to children. Today, drink a cup of tea as tea for five minutes! Experience its warmth, aroma, taste.
Life is not just about rushing to the next task. This moment is also life.

10. Kintsugi – Even broken things have new beauty.
In the Japanese art of 'Kintsugi', a broken clay pot is not thrown away. Its cracks are joined in a special way, and those cracks themselves are made a part of its beauty. Our life is also like that. Failures, pains, mistakes, lost opportunities – all these are the cracks in our lives. We don't have to hide them. We can learn from them. Instead of saying 'my life has cracks', say 'I have become stronger through those cracks'.

Final word: Change is not from tomorrow, but from today! After reading these ten Japanese techniques, there is no need to start everything from day one. Choose one today.g

Walk for ten minutes in the morning – Kaizen.
Give full attention to one task for 25 minutes – Pomodoro.
Do what makes you happy – Ikigai.
Walk among the trees for a while – Shinrin-yoku.
Remove unnecessary items – Osouji.
Accept your imperfections – Wabi-sabi.
Talk to family members with care – Omotenashi.

Gain new strength from old pains – Kintsugi.
