Forest Lamp Pandava's Wick

Sumalatha N

plant

Among the unique plants found in the forests of Malenadu, 'Pandavara Batti' or 'Aarti Gida' is one. This plant, which grows in the lap of nature, is a source of curiosity due to its special qualities.

It grows as a creeper. Its leaves are slightly thick, and since there are soft hairs on the back, it feels like velvet when touched. Small purple flowers bloom, and then seeds form in small cluster-like fruits. It is a bit difficult to grow this plant from seeds.

Its shoot can be taken, dipped in oil or smeared with oil, and lit like a lamp. The lamp burns for a long time.

Its specialty is that it feels as if nature itself has hidden a small lamp within it.

There is also a folk belief behind the name 'Pandavara Batti'.

In the Mahabharata, during their exile, when the Pandavas were wandering in the forest, they used parts of this plant with oil as torches for light, according to a belief among the people of Malenadu. It is said that the name 'Pandavara Batti' came from this reason.

There is also a belief that its shoots were used to light lamps in some rural temples. This plant is not just a wild creeper; it is a rare organism that combines folk belief, tradition, and the wonder of nature.

Many such rare plants are growing unnoticed in the forests around us. If the forest survives, such wonders of nature will also survive.
