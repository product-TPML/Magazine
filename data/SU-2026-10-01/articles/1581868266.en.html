The danger of an inactive account

Remaining investment

Girish C.G. Chartered Accountant

We open accounts in banks such as savings accounts, current accounts, fixed deposits (FD), recurring deposit accounts (RD). Sometimes, due to reasons like job change, relocation, death, change of address or mobile number, bank accounts are not used for a long time.

Such accounts are considered Inoperative Accounts after a specific period. If not claimed by customers even then, such deposits can be transferred to the Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEAF) account as per RBI rules.

1. What is an inactive account?

If there is no transaction from the customer for a specific period, the bank considers that account as inactive. For example:

The customer not depositing/withdrawing any money in the savings account.

Not making transactions like NEFT/RTGS/IMPS.

Not using a debit card.

Not making payments or deposits.

No transactions by the customer through net or mobile banking.

In such cases, it is checked according to the rules. Only the crediting of interest to the account is not considered a transaction made by the customer. For this reason, one should not assume that the account is active.

What are the consequences of an inactive account?

Even if the account is inactive, the money in it belongs to the customer. The bank does not acquire ownership of the money just because the account is inactive. However, customers may face restrictions on some services or additional verifications. For example:

KYC verification when withdrawing money.

Process of reactivating the account.

Need to submit identity documents.

It is best for customers to regularly check accounts that have not been used for a long time.

Depositor Education Fund

Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEAF) means that as per RBI rules, money from eligible deposit accounts that have been inactive for more than 10 years in the bank and not claimed is transferred to DEAF. The purpose of this is not just to transfer money. Its main objective is to create financial awareness among depositors.

Even if the money is transferred to DEAF, the depositor does not lose the right to his money. Eligible depositors or legal heirs can claim through the concerned bank. Customers or their nominee relatives can claim the money by contacting the bank and submitting the necessary documents.

Investor's duty

A list of all bank accounts should be made and details recorded.

Enter nominee details correctly. This helps the nominee or relative to claim money in case of the member's death.

Update KYC details from time to time. If the address or mobile number has changed, it should be entered.

It is good to inform family members about the deposits in the bank.

Precautions to be taken by customers

There is a possibility of some frauds happening in the name of DEAF. Do not give any information to unknown persons: OTP, ATM PIN, net banking details, UPI PIN. There is no need to pay money to an unknown person to claim money from a bank account. Only the official contact system of the bank should be used.

An inactive account does not mean a lost account. The customer's right is not over just because it has been transferred to DEAF.

Investment is not just about keeping money in the bank. It is also the investor's responsibility to know where that money is, when it matures, who the nominee is, and how to claim it when needed. g
