‘A notice has been generated in your name as part of SIR. Come to the polling station and collect the notice – BLO’

This is an SMS received by Sushilamma from a village at the foot of Siddarabett in Tovinakeri, Tumakuru. Sushila, 38, is an ASHA worker who visits four to five villages every day to inquire about people's health and well-being. It is both her husband's village and her native place. The BLO who sent the SMS is her daily companion, an Anganwadi worker. As soon as the SMS arrived, she called the BLO for details, and when she went to the Gram Panchayat office, the notice reached her hands.

‘Hey... your parents have eight children. Seven of them are mapped to your father. That's why they gave a notice... Give your SSLC marks card, and then caste and income (certificate). I'll take care of it...’ This was the reassurance given to Sushilamma by the BLO.

Sushilamma's parents had eight children. The eldest had died in an accident years ago. The remaining seven children are between 38 and 29 years old. When SIR was conducted in 2002, none of these seven were voters. By 2000, Sushilamma's mother had died during her ninth childbirth. As part of SIR, all of Sushilamma's siblings were mapped to their father's name.

In the 'Special Integrated Revision of Electoral Rolls or SIR', which aims to exclude ineligible voters and retain only eligible ones, a filter has been used stating that 'no more than six children can be mapped to a single parent'. This means that even if a father and mother have more than six children, not all children can map themselves as their parents. Since Sushilamma's siblings mapped themselves to their father Ramappa, violating this rule formulated by the Election Commission of India, all seven of them have received notices.

‘To whom else should we map ourselves if not to our parents? Should we map ourselves to others for SIR?’ Sushilamma's question has no answer even from the ERO (Electoral Registration Officer).

‘Sushilamma's name is Sushila in her SSLC certificate. In the voter ID, it is Sushilamma. She needs to explain this discrepancy to the Election Commission. In the context of rural India, the SIR rule has been formulated blindly, ignoring the reality that a single parent can have eight to ten children. We don't understand which officer added this rule or what logic it contains. SIR has been formulated without understanding such nuances,’ says Sanju of the Anti-SIR Coalition.

Sanju's words make sense. This is not just Sushilamma's story. The stories of Siddarangaiah from Pemmanahalli, Tumakuru, Thimmarayi from Hosapalya, Nelamangala, and Museb from Ramanagara are no different. All of them are having their citizenship questioned because they mapped themselves to their parents. When we spoke to SIR victims, considering the question raised by Sanju, dozens of such stories unfolded. The harsh reality of India, where child marriage, polygamy, and couples having many children exist, was not considered when formulating the entire SIR rules, and this truth repeatedly hits us in the face. They cannot accept that the voting rights of those who have already voted four or five times are being snatched away. Moreover, these voters are in a state of confusion, not knowing what to do next or what will happen.

The story of Jalil Ahmed Sheikh's family from a village near Shirahatti is different. Jalil, his wife, three children, two daughters-in-law, one son-in-law, and three grandchildren make up a large family. In the 2002 voter list, only Jalil's name was present, not anyone else's from the family. Jalil's wife doesn't remember if she voted or if she was registered to vote. All three of Jalil's children mapped themselves to their father's name when filling out the enumeration form. Now, Jalil has received a notice. He has been categorized as a suspicious voter due to a discrepancy in his name.

In the 2002 voter list, his name was 'Jalilshaikh', while in the 2026 voter list, it is 'Jalil Shaikh'. Jalil's relative is the BLO. Both the BLO and the BLO's supervisor know that 'Jalilshaikh' and 'Jalil Shaikh' are the same person. However, according to the 'Logical Discrepancy' artificial intelligence used by the Election Commission of India to verify voter details, these two are different individuals. This AI has now generated a notice in Jalil's name and handed it to the Electoral Registration Officer. The AI, which categorized the father as a 'suspicious voter', is also questioning the citizenship of his three children who mapped themselves with Jalil. Now, all four have received notices and need to provide documents to prove their citizenship.

The BLO has handed Jalil a list of all the documents that can be provided. 'Pension slip issued by Central/State Government to government employees, caste certificate, passport, land or house allotment letter, birth registration certificate, SSLC marks card, permanent residence certificate, identity card issued by Government/Banks/Post Office/Authorities/Corporations before 1987, forest rights certificate, National Register of Citizens, family register prepared by State Government/Local Bodies'... these are the documents on the list.

Jalil's eldest son is a central government employee, having served 15 years in India Post. Holding the documents given by the BLO, he said, 'My father only has a ration card, Aadhaar card, voter EPIC card, and house and property documents. The house and property documents are still in my grandfather's name. Since my father was not a government employee, he doesn't receive any pension. As he never attended school, he doesn't have an SSLC marks card. Since no land/house was allotted by the government, he doesn't have a title deed. As he is not a forest dweller, he doesn't have a forest rights certificate. My father doesn't have any of the documents they are asking for. The commission is not accepting the documents we have. They say Aadhaar alone is not enough.'

‘We also have a 120-year-old sale deed for the land in my grandfather's name. We have pahani, revenue, notices, everything. But now, our citizenship, which has been here for four generations, is being questioned in the name of SIR. If we don't provide my father's documents, the names of our entire family will be removed from the final voter list. Doesn't the documentation provided by the central government when they gave me a job prove our citizenship? And why are we being forced into a situation where we have to prove our citizenship?’ he asked.

Jalil's family has applied for a permanent residence certificate issued by the state government. Similarly, the Tahsildar rejected the application submitted by Jalil's neighbor's family. If Jalil does not get a permanent residence certificate, he has to submit the documents he has to the ERO. It is up to the ERO's discretion to accept or reject them. If the ERO rejects Jalil's documents, all four members of his family will not remain on the final voter list.

Sisters Seeta and Geeta Kadanavar from Vadagaon, Belagavi, have received a notice. The notice states, 'The age difference between your sister and you is less than 9 months. Please explain the reason and provide appropriate documents.'

Seeta and Geeta are twin sisters.

‘We are twin sisters. We have mapped ourselves to our father's name. Is it possible for the age difference between those born within five or six minutes on the same day to be more than 9 months? Does the Election Commission not even have this common sense? When I called the BLO and told them about it, they said, 'Both of you come for the inquiry together. Bring your birth certificate.' Where does the birth certificate say that we are twins? I work here in Bengaluru. My sister runs a loom in Vadagaon. To answer such a ridiculous notice, I have to go from Bengaluru to Belagavi,’ Seeta expressed her anger.

Puttalakshmamma from Swarnasandra, Mandya, has also received a notice. Puttalakshmamma's mother, Narasamma, is now 67 years old. Puttalakshmamma is 53. The age difference between them is 14 years and seven months. The notice states, 'The age difference between your mother and you is less than 15 years. Provide appropriate documents for this.'

‘My mother was married at 13. I was born in her 14th year. I don't know my mother's real age. I don't know my real age either. Earlier, when getting a voter card, when the officials asked for her age, my mother said, 'She got married at 13, and this girl was born the next year.' The officials themselves gave us both different birth dates. They put January 1, 1959, for my mother and July 1, 1972, for me. Now they are asking for a birth certificate or SSLC marks card. We don't have either of them. Where should we go?’ asks Puttalakshmi, an activist working for an organization for the rights of civic workers.

There are thousands of people whose names have been excluded from the draft voter list even though all their documents were correct and they had submitted the enumeration form. Deepak from Rajarajeshwarinagar, Bengaluru, has been living in his own flat for 12 years. Deepak, his wife, father, and mother have filled and submitted their enumeration forms with mapping details. The concerned BLO came to Deepak's house and collected the enumeration form. However, in the draft voter list, all his family members' names are present except Deepak's. Deepak has been categorized as 'permanently shifted' and excluded from the draft voter list. The BLO is saying that Deepak's enumeration form is not available. Deepak has now been forced to submit application form-6. Along with this, he has signed a declaration stating, 'I am registering as a voter for the first time.'

The error of BLOs categorizing living voters as 'dead' occurred in Kanchaghatta village, Tiptur. Several voters from the same polling station were mistakenly added to the ASDDO list as 'dead'. After a complaint was filed, the error was corrected. In Govindaraj Nagar constituency, Bengaluru, 160 living voters were similarly categorized as 'dead'. It is ironic that all of them had to stand before the polling officers to prove that they were alive.

If all the voters mentioned in the above story fail to prove their citizenship, their names will not be on the final voter list. They will all have to sign a declaration stating, 'I am registering as a voter for the first time,' while lying and asserting, 'This is true.' The 43.81 lakh people who will receive notices and the 65 lakh voters who have been excluded as permanently shifted fall under the purview of this signature. The fact that more than one crore voters in the state have to sign in this manner is proof of the severity of the crisis brought about by SIR.

‘Those without documents are not Indians’

‘Earlier, when identifying illegal immigrants, it was checked whether they had documents. Under SIR, that has been reversed. SIR has changed 'those who are not Indians do not have documents' to 'those who do not have documents are not Indians'. If these two sentences are understood correctly, it becomes clear how SIR views Indians suspiciously,’ says Mutturaj of Jagrutha Karnataka, expressing his concern.

If the question is raised whether Indians should have all these documents, the dry, emotionless, and decisive answer from Election Commission officials is, 'Indians will have at least one of these 11 documents. Don't I have it, don't you have it? If there are no documents, it means they are not Indians.' But the harsh truth that there are Indians among us, in our villages, in our streets, and even in our homes who do not have any of the documents requested by the Election Commission repeatedly confronts us when SIR is pursued.

‘Do not disqualify those who have changed homes’

As part of SIR in the state, approximately 65 lakh voters have been excluded from the draft voter list as 'permanently shifted voters'. The Election Commission has accepted that all of them are voters. If so, they should have been given an opportunity to amend their addresses. Form-8 should have been collected from them to update their addresses. Instead, they have been excluded from the draft voter list. All their previous records of voting rights have been erased. If they all want to be included in the voter list again, they have to sign a declaration stating, 'I am registering as a voter for the first time.' For whom is the Election Commission playing this drama of removing previous voters and then re-enrolling them with false declarations?

–Prakash Raj, Actor (Question raised at a discussion at Bengaluru Press Club on September 12)
