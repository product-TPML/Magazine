The bilingual film 'Dayara', starring Malayalam actor Prithviraj Sukumaran and Hindi actress Kareena Kapoor, was recently released. Director Meghna Gulzar had convinced both of them to act with the intention of releasing the film in both Hindi and Malayalam.

The Hindi film industry is not new to Prithviraj. He had acted in the Hindi film 'Aiyyaa' with Rani Mukerji in 2012. After acting in films like Aurangzeb, Naam Shabana, Bade Miyan Chote Miyan, Sarzameen, he became well-known in this industry.

This time, during the film's promotion, he faced an important question. The question was, 'What is the difference between the Hindi film industry and the Malayalam film industry?' This question was based on the fact that there are a greater number of good films in Malayalam.

Prithviraj's answer to this question was detailed and interesting: 'In Malayalam, screenwriters first call actors or directors to tell them that such a subject exists. Then they ask for time to discuss. If the screenplay is liked, the work of finding producers begins. Sometimes, the storytellers themselves directly inform the producers about the storyline. The storytellers there are capable. They are important in the process of making a film. However, in the Hindi film industry, the entire system has become corporatized. Here, screenwriters do not get to interact directly with actors or directors. Agencies intervene. If they agree, discussions with specific actors are possible. If they don't agree, they are not accessible. There is a tradition where agencies handle financial negotiations. In Malayalam, the budget now exceeds ₹5-6 crores. Hundreds of crores are invested to make films. Even then, the importance of writers has not diminished. There was a time when people applauded when they saw the names of Salim-Javed on screen in Hindi films. It's not happening like that now.'

Kareena, who was silently listening to Prithviraj's answer, nodded as if everything was true. It is indeed a remarkable phenomenon that a prominent South Indian actor-producer directly shed light on the real problem of the Hindi film industry in this way.g

Raveena-Karisma's memory beyond their differences

Karisma was sitting on the judge's chair in a dance reality show. Raveena Tandon had come as a guest. The words of dance director Saroj Khan, who had previously said that there was a rift between these two actresses, had widely circulated on social media. Hence, there was curiosity about how the two would talk now.

In the 1994 Hindi film 'Andaz Apna Apna', Salman Khan, Aamir Khan and these two actresses had acted together. Raveena recalled an incident from the shooting of that time.

'The four of us didn't talk much to each other. There was a scene where Karisma and I were tied to a pole. We both stood in that tied state. The others went out for lunch. We were extremely hungry. Then both our ropes were untied. I then asked Karisma if she wanted juice. From there, our conversation started. We all started talking and laughing during the shooting of the song 'Yeh Raat Aur Yeh Doori',' she recalled the old incident.

When Raveena opened her memory box, Karisma's face was also filled with a smile.
