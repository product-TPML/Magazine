Nagaraj, the paddy variety conservator

Nagaraj Naik

Rahul Belagali

Do you grow paddy? Where is the field? Which village and how far do you have to go? If you ask such questions, you will get hundreds of answers from farmers. They mention many names like Raichur, various districts of the state including Uttara Kannada, villages in Andhra Pradesh, villages near Chennai. If you extend the same question a bit and ask, 'Do you know about the person who grew 600 rare varieties of paddy?', it is difficult to get a clear answer. Because, they are not well known. They have spoken little about their achievements in paddy.

Yes! Such a person lives in a village called Kagala in Kumta taluk of Uttara Kannada district. His name is Nagaraj Naik. A pure farmer, he has dedicated his land to growing paddy and conducting unique experiments. Having developed a great interest in diverse varieties of paddy, he has not focused on anything else for 15 years. He has grown 600 varieties of paddy in the same field. He is engaged in the work of explaining and preserving its importance.

Nagaraj Naik observed that farmers in most villages of Uttara Kannada district were going to Goa, Mangaluru or Bengaluru to find other jobs and build their lives. If everyone leaves for the big cities like this, who will stay in the village? What should happen to agriculture? This question bothered him. He thought a lot. He reviewed all the pros and cons. Finally, he decided, 'I will stay in the village. I will do agricultural work.'

Standing in front of his five-acre land, he asked himself, 'What all can be done' rather than 'What can be done in this small land'. In the initial days, he grew 'Kagga' paddy in two acres of land. He planned to grow various types of paddy in the remaining three acres. He traveled across the country. He visited villages. He brought paddy seeds from there, planted them in his field, and grew them. In no time, the number of varieties increased from 400 to 600. This led to the conservation of various species that were on the verge of extinction and partially disappearing.

If you visit Nagaraj Naik's agricultural land, you will not only see a beautiful green environment. You will also see a bank of various seeds. He has built a small house next to the land and has given it the beautiful name 'Community Based Seed Bank'. Its sole goal and objective is the indigenization of seeds and the development of paddy varieties. All types of seeds are preserved. They are taken care of so that they do not spoil. They are preserved very neatly and delicately.

If you visit this bank, you can see white Kagga, black Kagga varieties along with Jyoti, Mahaveer, Rajamudi, Jayapadma, Salem, Govadhana species, Rajabhoga, Nagabhatta, Shakti, Palagara, Ratnachooda, Bhoothanatha, Mysore Mallige, Kaje Jaya, red, brown, medicinal paddy along with paddy seeds that grow in salt water and deep water. You can learn their scientific names. Nagaraj Naik himself explains the origin of the seed, the benefits of the variety, and the factors that are beneficial for health.

Interestingly, Nagaraj Naik's farming method is completely organic. He does not use chemical fertilizers or chemical elements for any reason. Believing that chemical fertilizers harm the crop, he says, 'Our nature and land are very pure. But we humans are destroying not only the crop but also the land by excessively using chemical fertilizers, pesticides, etc. Our health deteriorates by consuming food grown with chemical fertilizers. Due to continuous use of chemical fertilizers, the land also gradually loses its fertility.'

Nagaraj Naik is not limited to growing paddy. He prepares and sells honey using organic methods. He prepares coconut oil. He provides good food that can help those suffering from various health problems, including diabetes, to improve their health.

'I encourage farmers to grow various varieties of paddy not only in my land but also in other lands. I provide them with necessary guidance. I make them realize that chemical fertilizers and pesticides are not the only solutions for everything. If we adopt organic methods in agriculture, it is good for us and for the earth. Everyone should understand this. First of all, we should give up greed. We should cultivate patience. Only then can we live safely. We can also protect the environment,' says Nagaraj Naik.
