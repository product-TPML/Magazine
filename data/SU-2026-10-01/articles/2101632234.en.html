Even though forts are the surviving parts of our history, turning the pages of their history reveals stories of the rise and fall of several dynasties, intrigues, conspiracies, love, and hatred. They mirror the courage, shrewdness, and foresight of the people of that era, and also possess designs and technologies that challenge even today's technologically advanced people. Forts, which are dear to researchers and historians, become beloved to trekkers if they are on a hill.

One such fort is the 'Rajagiri Fort' in Tamil Nadu. It is part of Gingee Fort. Rajagiri, Krishnagiri, and Chakkilidurg - these three forts are located on three hills and are connected to each other in a triangular shape. This complex of three forts is famous as 'Gingee Fort'. For Tamils, it is Senjee Fort. Among these three forts, Rajagiri Fort is the tallest. As the name of the fort suggests, it is the king's fort and is considered important. Krishnagiri is the queen's fort, and Chakkilidurg is like the servants' fort.

Although this fort is in Tamil Nadu, it appears to be a part of the history of undivided India. It was under the rule of the Cholas of Tamil Nadu, the Vijayanagara kings of Karnataka, the Marathas, the Mughals, the French, and the British! It stands as an example of the efficiency of the people of that time, even without facilities, privileges, technology, and communication. From this, one can imagine the influence of the fort.

Nothing is permanent...

The history of the fort dates back to the 9th century. Initially, an observation tower was built on the hill by the Chola kings. In 1200 AD, Ananda Kon of the Konar dynasty built this fort.

The influence of the fort reached its peak in the 15th century, during the rule of the Vijayanagara kings. The fort was under the control of the Nayaks of Vijayanagara. Initially, its name was 'Ananda Betta'. Its name was changed to 'Rajagiri' by Tupaki Krishnappa Nayak. Krishnappa Nayak is said to have built the Kalyana Mahal, granaries, and other structures here.

No matter how powerful, nothing is permanent. As the influence of the Vijayanagara kings declined, the fort came under the control of the Bijapur Sultans in the mid-17th century. As the Marathas took over the northern and southern states, the fort came under Maratha rule. It was then under Mughal control for 7-8 years. When French rule began in Pondicherry, the fort became French property. As a symbol of victory, the French are said to have removed some pillars of the fort and installed them in Pondicherry.

In the late 18th century, it came under British control. The British did capture the fort. But it was left as it was. They did not bother with its preservation. Even though it was a stone fort, it slowly began to deteriorate.

As India gained independence in 1947, the Archaeological Survey of India began its restoration work. In 2025, Gingee Fort was recognized as a World Heritage Site (UNESCO World Heritage Site). In short, the fort became part of history as a site of fierce battles.

Trekking in Rajagiri...

Rajagiri is located in the Villupuram district of Tamil Nadu. Rajagiri, visible along the highway from Pondicherry and Tiruvannamalai, is just a few kilometers inland from the highway. It is 150 km from Chennai.

The total height of the hill is 800 feet, the height of the fort is 25 feet, and the width is 5 feet. To climb the hill and reach the top of the fort, one has to climb about 1100 steps. The stone steps are not uniform; some places are slippery. Also, it is not entirely a path of steps. There are muddy paths in some places. In between, one can see grand entrances and guard rooms. This is a refreshing place for trekkers. As trekkers climb up, the view of Krishnagiri Fort on the opposite side, and the green fields and trees below, soothe the eyes and mind. Even though the sun is hot, a strong wind also blows.

Those who cannot trek can still get a lot of information by walking around the lower level of the fort and can see several buildings that narrate history.

About an hour and a half is enough to climb up, and forty-five minutes to descend. The fort gates open at 9 AM. Will the Tamil Nadu sun not burn us? So trekkers should not forget to carry water. Also, there is a lot of monkey trouble, so it is better to carry a thick stick.

Buildings inside the fort...

The fort has two entrances. One is Pondicherry Gate, and the other is Vellore Gate. At one time, there was a canal around the fort. Now, no water is visible in the canal. After crossing the fort entrance, there is a not-so-wide 'S' shaped path in English. Grass is grown on both sides, and broken idols found in the fort itself are arranged on it.

In the spacious courtyard inside the fort, banyan trees offer shade and comfort to the tired, welcoming and soothing them. There are several buildings on the lower level of the fort. Some are in good condition, some are dilapidated. Although the buildings do not have much architectural decoration to speak of, they reflect the past glory. The buildings here must have been built by different dynasties in different periods, so they are in different styles.

There is a stone building, which, though not a palace, is believed to have been inhabited by the king's family. Outside, one can see a place for public gatherings. The granite stone throne here, with its cylindrical, pillow-like stone backrest, attracts attention. The spacious granary here can rival today's well-equipped warehouses. Not just for months, but enough food items for years could be stored here. Such precautions were taken against floods, droughts, and enemy attacks. Its roof has three hemispherical decorations.

Places to see

Kalyana Mahal: This is a seven-storied building. Next to it is a long hall with several pillars. There are no doors or windows. It was apparently used for festivals and public celebrations. This building was built during the rule of the Vijayanagara kings. It is also in the same style. I remember seeing such a long hall in Hampi. It was used for trade there.

Observation Tower: Located at the top of the hill, it crowns the entire fort. The stone tower has several floors.

Elephant Pond: Perhaps elephants used to bathe in this spacious pond at one time, which is why it must have been named so. The pond has steps and a stone wall. Next to it are the elephant stable and horse stable.

Gymnasium: The gymnasium is spacious and still in good condition. Soldiers must have been trained here, as there was always some war going on!

Kamalakanni Amman Temple: This is a small temple. Since the door was closed, it was not visible what was inside. There are Ganesh idols on either side of the door. To the left of the temple is a Shiva Linga. In front of the temple are a long stone with some carvings and tridents. From a board nearby, it was learned that human sacrifices and buffalo sacrifices used to take place here at one time. A painting on the rock behind the temple attracts attention. The colors used for it are natural and have not faded even after hundreds of years. In the painting, Vishnu, Bhudevi, and Sridevi can be seen. Another painting nearby is not clear. There is also a large lake nearby. Even today, a festival is held here every year, and people from nearby villages come.

Bala Ranganatha Temple: A small temple. The spire with many idol figures is beautiful, but there is no idol inside. It is found after completing about half of the trek. Another Ranganatha temple is next to the observation tower. An important point to note here is that it has a kalasha (finial) on top of its roof instead of a shikhara (spire).

Another interesting thing is the system made for storing guns, ammunition, and gunpowder. It is in the basement. A small opening is at the top. Also, large cannons can be seen in some places. The boards of the Archaeological Survey of India placed here and there provide a lot of information.

Venkataramana Temple, Shiva and Devi Temple, Sadatullah Khan Mosque, jail, and lakes named Chettikulam and Chakraikulam are on another side of the fort.
