You may have noticed news like 'Rescue of child laborers as per Chief Minister's instructions. 800 child laborers taken into custody in a single day' in daily newspapers and TV media.

Yes. In the second week of September, the Chief Minister of Karnataka noticed child laborers in the Yashwantpur market in Bengaluru. He instructed the concerned labor department officials and police to rescue those children. On that day, 13 children were rescued. The Chief Minister also said, 'The rescue of working children should not stop here, rescue working children all over the state.' As a result, by the end of the second week of September, officials and police jointly raided many establishments and commercial places across the state and rescued 800 children.

India, which has agreed to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, has guaranteed compulsory education for all children up to 14 years of age. It has implemented the Right to Free and Compulsory Education for Children Act. There is also the 'Children and Adolescent Labor System (Prohibition and Regulation) Act' to prevent children from dropping out of school and falling into labor. According to this, no child below 14 years of age can be engaged in any kind of labor. Children between 14 and 18 years of age cannot be engaged in hazardous industries such as factories, mines, fisheries, chemical and other toxic substance manufacturing and usage.

If anyone disregards these laws and employs children, a complaint should be filed with Childline 1098, considering the working children as 'children in need of care and protection'. Local women and child protection committees, local police, labor officials, and district child protection officers should come forward to rescue the children. These children should be presented before the District Child Welfare Committee. In addition, a police complaint will be filed to take legal action against those who were exploiting the children.

The Child Welfare Committee talks to the children, understands their family background, formulates a rehabilitation plan for them, and instructs the family, school, and local government to follow it. If the children are from another district or state, the child welfare committee there is contacted and directed to take rehabilitation measures. They are temporarily kept in 'child care homes' and helped to reunite with their families, along with health, nutritious food, and education.
