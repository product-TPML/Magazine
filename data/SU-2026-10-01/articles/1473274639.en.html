Gireesha Doddamani

‘The seam of the ball released by Mohammed Shami travels straight. It doesn't wobble anywhere in the air. But the way the ball swings out or in after hitting the ground is unique. The wicket falls before the batter can react. This is not just skill. It's an amazing art...’

This is what Wasim Akram, known as the 'Sultan of Swing' from Pakistan, once said about India's Shami's bowling in his commentary. Kapil Dev, Chetan Sharma, and West Indies' Michael Holder, among other famous personalities, have also been impressed by Shami's bowling. There was a time when Jasprit Bumrah attacked from one end and Shami from the other. But for the past two years, Bumrah alone has been handling the responsibility. The search for a fast bowler who can provide him with adequate support is still ongoing. Some bowlers, including Mukesh Kumar, Prasidh Krishna, Arshdeep Singh, Akash Deep, and Anshul Kamboj, did attract attention. But it will take a long time for all of them to reach Shami's level. Among them, Mohammed Siraj has shown more promise. He also doesn't get many opportunities.

Recently, Shami achieved a 'century' in first-class cricket by playing the Duleep Trophy final in Chennai. That is; he achieved the feat of playing his 100th first-class match. Before that, he had bowled brilliantly in the Duleep Trophy quarter-final and semi-final held in Bengaluru. Shami's role was crucial in the East Zone team winning the trophy after 13 years. However, the 36-year-old Shami has not been given a chance in the squad for the upcoming series against West Indies. Because of this, it seems that the time for his international cricket career to come to an end is approaching.

But the question arises whether the Indian team's bowling department will be balanced in next year's ODI World Cup tournament without Shami.

After all, Shami last played in the 2025 Champions Trophy final; he has not appeared in ODI cricket since. It has been three years since he played a Test match. He has not played an international match in the T20 format for a year. After recovering from injuries and surgeries, he has proven his ability in the matches he has played in the IPL and domestic tournaments. Yet, the selection committee headed by Ajit Agarkar has not considered Shami. But Shami is still bowling with his usual full strength. His skill has not diminished. In the Duleep Trophy final held in the scorching heat of Chennai, he bowled in short spells and troubled the South Zone batsmen.

Last year, his swing magic in the IPL also attracted attention. His experience, having played for the Indian team since 2013, is also evident in his bowling now. The 229 wickets he has taken in 65 Tests also came mostly in crucial situations. He has taken 206 wickets in 108 ODIs and 27 wickets in 25 T20 matches. There is no doubt that he is a match-winning player, bowling at an economy of 5.58 in ODI cricket. He also holds the distinction of being the fastest bowler to take 200 wickets, having bowled 5126 balls in ODI cricket. Throughout his career, he has faced injury problems many times. But he never gave up and repeatedly regained his place in the team.

Even during the COVID period, he practiced by setting up nets in his village. But in the last five years, he has not lost his rhythm despite the pressure of family conflict. He has achieved full concentration and proved his strength in the opportunities he got. Yet, it is surprising that the selection committee has not paid attention to him. Especially since Bumrah has also not played many matches recently due to fitness and back pain issues. In such a situation, the team needs an experienced bowler like Shami. It is not appropriate to exclude Shami, who has good fitness and skill, due to age. If he is in the team, young bowlers will have a lot to learn. After all, Shami is not just a bowler, but a determined fighter who fights until the last moment for victory.
