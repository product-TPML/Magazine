KPSC needs rejuvenation

Victory

Rajesh Rai Chatla

The Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC), an autonomous, constitutional body that selects eligible candidates for civil services, including the 'Karnataka Administrative Service' (KAS), one of the highest posts in the state's administrative sector, is once again in the news. The former examination controller has been jailed, and the chairman has been suspended. This is the first such development in KPSC's history.

Work is underway to rejuvenate KPSC. The commission, led by current secretary K. Jyothi, examination controller H. Prasanna, and interim chairman B. Prabhudeva, has moved to restore transparency, strict discipline, and trust in the government recruitment process. It has decided to initiate significant reform measures. This move is a relief for the state's youth.

The D.K. Shivakumar-led government has planned a massive project to fill more than 72,000 vacant posts in various departments through KPSC and the Karnataka Examinations Authority (KEA) in the coming days. The government's objective is that this recruitment process should proceed smoothly without any irregularities, question paper leaks, or technical glitches. The government has formed a high-level review committee chaired by retired Chief Secretary S.V. Ranganath. Examination controller H. Prasanna has put forward four principles for reform in the examination system. His thinking is that significant change is possible if KPSC adopts these principles.

The competitive examinations conducted by KPSC are OMR sheet-based. The first proposal is to change the design of the OMR sheet itself to conduct the recruitment process with utmost transparency, without any scope for irregularities. The sheet currently in use has four circles for shading the correct answer. A fifth circle should be added. Candidates must compulsorily shade the 5th circle for unanswered questions. Immediately after the exam, all candidates' OMR sheets should be scanned and uploaded to the website. Following that, the correct answers should be published. When the results are declared, the names, registration numbers, categories, and marks of all candidates who appeared for the exam should be disclosed. Through this, the examination controller argues that it is possible to bring reliability to competitive examinations and transparency to selection. The commission has approved these proposals. It has decided to apply these principles to all future recruitment notifications.

To bring reforms in KPSC, the then government formed the P.C. Hota Committee in 2013. Only some of the recommendations made by that committee have been implemented. However, the expected change in the system has not occurred. The House Committee, comprising members of the Legislative Assembly led by Congress' R.V. Deshpande, is expected to submit its report in the upcoming session.

Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar has formed a committee chaired by S.V. Ranganath to submit a report on the measures to be taken to review the reforms to be brought in competitive examinations and recruitment processes. Retired IAS officers T.M. Vijayabhaskar, Vandita Sharma, and the Chief Minister's economic advisor L.K. Ateeq are on the committee. This committee is responsible for making necessary recommendations to make competitive examinations and the recruitment system transparent and reliable.

Lakhs of people across the state are studying day and night with the dream of getting a government job. KPSC must instill confidence that eligible candidates will not be wronged for any reason and that there is no room for any doubt or distrust. KPSC has the responsibility to conduct recruitment very professionally by properly utilizing technology. A transparent process needs to be completed and a model demonstrated.
