An Essay Series of Enlightened Minds

Manjushree Kadakola

The Melody of Stations

Sudhakar Devadiga B.

Mahati

Just as a fragrance wafting in the air during a stroll, the chirping of birds, the warmth of sunbeams, or the smile of a stranger can fill the heart and delight the mind, a good light essay can easily enter the minds of sensitive readers and bring joy. Reading a good light essay is an experience of relishing our own memories and provides a new perspective to our experiences. Such an experience also occurs when reading the essays in Sudhakar Devadiga's collection 'Niladanagala Nadalile' (The Melody of Stations).

Sudhakar Devadiga is a Kannada lecturer by profession. He has attracted the attention of sensitive readers through his profound notes of subtle insights. 'Odina Hangu' is his collection of critical writings. There are examples in Kannada where critics have yearned to enhance the brilliance of their creativity through essays. Giraddi Govindaraj, Narahalli Balasubrahmanya, Rahamat Tarikere, and recently H.S. Satyanarayana have attracted the attention of sensitive readers through their essays. Sudhakar Devadiga is a new addition to this list. In fact, Devadiga has a strong background for his love of essays. His Ph.D. study was on essays. That study was published under the name 'Uḷida Akasha' (The Remaining Sky). The result of his academic interest turning towards essay writing is 'Niladanagala Nadalile' (The Melody of Stations).

The striking similarity in the fourteen compositions here, at first glance, is their attractive titles. Titles like 'Beliya Huvugala Savira Nila Bagegannu' (The Thousand Blue Eyes of Fence Flowers), 'Niladanagala Nadalile' (The Melody of Stations), 'Marevu Nenapugalemba Atmasakharu' (Forgetfulness and Memories as Soulmates), 'Kukkuta Panchanga' (Cock's Almanac) invite one to read. These titles also indicate the essayist's love for literature.

The charm of light essays lies in the richness of details and the cleverness in weaving those details. Anyone can compile details with a little effort. The challenge for the essayist arises in creating an organic relationship between those details and in making something new dawn on the readers through those details. If the selected details do not receive the touch of the essayist's identity, those details give the feeling of accumulating unnecessary items as trash in the house. In the hands of a good essayist, those object details are neatly arranged and become a work of art. The writings in the collection 'Niladanagala Nada Leele' (The Melody of Stations) begin to grow in the minds of sensitive readers by incorporating the essayist's readings and insights, thus acquiring an essayistic voice.

As an example of Sudhakar's skill as an essayist, one can observe the writing 'Marevu Nenapugalemba Atmasakharu' (Forgetfulness and Memories as Soulmates). This essay beautifully narrates the objects 'forgetfulness' and 'memory' which greatly influence life. The essay illustrates with examples how these two can manifest in various situations of life. This essay has acquired several dimensions on the foundation of family relationships, in the background of mental processes, and against the backdrop of social and literary contexts. From the embarrassing incident caused by the forgetfulness of a forgetful professor to the legend related to Kirtinath Kurtakoti's memory power, this essay contains many narratives. Thus, this essay, which grows deliciously by including many interesting incidents, also includes memories of humiliation. The philosophy of the essay, which states, 'Even those with immense memory power will have a shadow of forgetfulness. But it remains invisible, not falling into our sight in the brilliance of light. Even in those enveloped by a considerable amount of forgetfulness, there will be rays of light. It may just not be prominently visible,' and the realization, 'These are like soulmates with one's hand on the other's shoulder. They walk together. Unaware of this, we make the mistake of separating and differentiating them,' bring a special brilliance to the essay. The essay 'Dayeyemba Dharmada Moola' (The Root of Religion is Compassion) sadly states the truth that in modern times, humans are moving away from coexistence. The coexistence here applies to humans, and also to humans and other living beings; it is also an anxiety about humans moving away from nature. This sadness is also present in the essay 'Beliya Huvugala Savira Nila Bagegannu' (The Thousand Blue Eyes of Fence Flowers).

'Niladanagala Nadalile' (The Melody of Stations), which meditates on the many possibilities of bus stations and the life-affirming moments that take shape there, is an essay that attracts attention with its lively writing. In the essay 'Ninta Gadiyara' (The Stopped Clock), time itself takes the essayist by the hand and leads him, and also engages in conversation with us. The essays 'Manada Koleya Toleyaballode' (If the Dirt of the Mind Can Be Washed Away), 'Matte Male Huyyutide' (It's Raining Again), 'Varshakalada Pushpa' (Flower of the Rainy Season) have acquired a tender quality due to their vocal power and the author's social concern. This essay series is a welcome addition to the dwindling essay genre. l
