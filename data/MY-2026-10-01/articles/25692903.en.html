The Shadow of the Mahatma

Art: Santhosh Sasihithlu

The poet D. Govinda Das of the Navodaya period was in the news in his environment for having met Mahatma Gandhi and touched him. Many were thrilled to touch the poet who had touched the Mahatma. Perhaps, in the Indian context, a personality who touched society like Gandhiji, and continues to touch it in many ways even now, is extremely rare. Even for those who stay away from Gandhiji, when the question of his existence arises, the Mahatma is the support and the address. The 'Shadow of the Mahatma', which encompasses the present like the conscience of the nation, is also dense in the writings of this issue. One of the special features of this issue is an article remembering the poet Govinda Das, who, despite being touched by Gandhiji, was neglected by the literary world. This article brings to light many subtleties that sensitive readers should note.

Two other special features of this issue are an article commemorating the centenary of a newspaper and printing press that worked to build Kannada with Hyderabad Karnataka as its center, and an article based on a conversation with film still photographer 'Pragati' Ashwathanarayana. We believe that these articles, which showcase many facets of Kannada culture, will be enjoyed by 'Mayura readers' in the form of a variety of tastes.

As always, this issue is rich with experimental stories, poems, essays, and book reviews.
