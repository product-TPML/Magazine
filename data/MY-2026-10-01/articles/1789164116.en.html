Siddavanahalli Krishnasharma

Tulsidas said: 'Compassion is the root of Dharma, and arrogance is the root of sin.' This seems to me to be a scientific truth, as true as two plus two equals four. The strength of compassion is the strength of the soul; that is Satyagraha, and its evidence is found at every step. If this strength were not present, the earth would have sunk into the netherworld long ago. But you ask for historical evidence. Let's first see what history means. History (iti+ha+asa) means 'it was like this, it happened like this'. If this is the meaning of history, then countless examples of Satyagraha can be given.

If the stories of kings are history, then evidence of Satyagraha cannot be found in them. Can silver be found in a tin mine? The history we know is the story of the world's wars. That's why there is a proverb in English: a country without history is a happy country. How kings played, how they became enemies, how they killed each other, all this is described in current history. If this were all that happened in the world, this world would have drowned long ago. If the history of the world began with war, not a single human being would be alive today. No one who was immersed in war has survived. The indigenous people of Australia were destroyed in the same way. Remember that these inhabitants did not use Satyagraha for self-defense. The current inhabitants of Australia may also meet the same fate, there is no doubt about it. There is a proverb that those who wield the sword die by the sword, and swimmers die in water.

The very fact that there are still billions of people in the world is proof that this world is not run by brute force, but by soul force. Soul force is an undeniable proof of success. What more is needed than the fact that despite all these wars, the world still exists and thrives?

Millions of people in the world live by believing in this soul force. Disputes and quarrels in millions of families end due to its influence. Hundreds of communities live in peace. The history you speak of does not notice this, nor can it. If anything obstructs the flow of kindness and love, it is your history. Two brothers quarrel, one of them repents and awakens the love within him, and both live in peace again. No one notices this. But if those brothers, due to lawyers or other reasons, develop enmity and fight with weapons, or go to court, this is another form of brute force. Then this matter appears in newspapers; neighbors talk about it. It can also appear in history. What is true for a family is true for nations. There is not one justice for a family and another for a nation. The report of unnatural acts contrary to nature has now become history. Satyagraha is natural, therefore there is no mention of it in history.

Satyagraha is the practice of gaining one's rights by enduring hardship oneself. If I say I will not do work that I do not like, it is like exercising soul force. For example, a law made by the government applies to me. I don't like that law. If I wage war against the government and have that law removed, I have used brute force. If I refuse to accept that law and suffer punishment for disregarding it, that is Satyagraha. There is self-sacrifice in this.

If this Satyagraha is used for injustice, only the person who uses it suffers. No one else suffers for his mistake. Many things that people do are later found to be wrong. No one can say, 'Mine is right, that is wrong, my opinion is definite.' If he is certain that something is wrong, he should not do it. He must endure all the hardships that come from not doing it. This is the key to Satyagraha.

***

He who understands the concept of humanity, who fears only God, fears no one else. Man-made laws are not inevitable for him. The government also does not expect this from us. The government does not say, 'You must do this.' It says, 'If you don't do this, we will punish you.' Whatever the law says, we believe it is our duty, our dharma, to do it. If one realizes that obeying an unjust law is detrimental to one's manhood, no oppression can bind him. This is the path to Swaraj. l

(Selected excerpts from Mahatma Gandhi's 'Hind Swaraj', translated into Kannada by Siddavanahalli Krishnasharma)
