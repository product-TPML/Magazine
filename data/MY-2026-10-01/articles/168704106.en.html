Veerashaiva Dharma Sanjeevini

Devu Pattar

In what was formerly known as 'Hyderabad Karnataka' and now 'Nizam Karnataka', nothing is important. Or everything is unimportant. In the face of distress and helplessness, joy and celebration naturally recede. Thus, if there is one policy and rule for the whole of Karnataka, it is another for this region. The events recorded or unrecorded in the pages of history are not merely narratives of distress or pain. Those pages also contain an equal measure of success and achievements. And that is a natural process. If one observes, notes, and records it, only then will it be understood that the past was not just a story of lament. Exactly two years ago, the first Kannada newspaper of 'Hyderabad Karnataka', 'Veerashaiva Dharma Sanjeevini', completed a hundred years since its inception (1924). Karnataka, let alone Hyderabad Karnataka, did not even remember it. This newspaper was started by Nayakallu Basavaraja Shastri and Masigi Basavaraja Shastri. It was a monthly magazine dedicated to religious matters. That does not diminish its
importance. Not only a hundred years ago, but even now, most activities are centered around religion and God.

Naikal/Naykal is a small village on the way from Shahapur to Yadgir. Basavaraja Shastri, originally a resident of Naykal, belonged to the Godyal/Godihal Math in Shahapur taluk. Basavaraja Shastri, a student of the Sanskrit पाठशाला (Pathashala) in Solapur, was interested in writing, especially in Kannada writing. He was joined by Maski (Masagi) Basavaraja Shastri, who shared his name. Together, they ventured into the newspaper business. Looking at the few available issues, their hard work and struggles are evident. It was an adventurous undertaking to work for Kannada in a region where Urdu and Marathi were dominant, both politically and commercially.The 'Veerashaiva Dharma Sanjeevini' newspaper, which started in Raichur, later continued its publication as 'Dharma Sanjeevini' from Yadgir. A printing press was also started specifically for publishing this newspaper. Nayikal Basavaraja Shastri was responsible for this, and Maski Basavaraja Shastri supported him. This newspaper ran for ten years. Senior journalist B. Mahadevappa, in his article 'Sharana Samskruti mattu Patrikegalu' (2003, 'Alaru'), documented that 'in the second decade of the 20th century, Naykal Basavaraja Shastri and Maski Basavaraja Shastri, who were favorite students of Varada Mallappa, who had started the Solapur Sanskrit Veda Pathashala, established the Shankarananda Press in Yadgir specifically for the newspaper and ran the 'Dharma Sanjeevini' monthly.'

The first issue of 'Veerashaiva Dharma Sanjeevini' was published in the month of Karthika of the Rakthakshi Samvatsara in 1924. It was printed at the
'Model Mudrakshara Shala' in Bangalore. In this issue, the editor, Nayikallu Basavaraja Shastri, wrote an editorial titled 'Patrika Svavishaya' (About the Newspaper). Maski S. Basavappa Shastri was recorded as the publisher. The language and content of this editorial are intriguing.

'It is true that people of various communities in all parts of the world are striving to develop their respective mother tongues; we directly observe that the Andhra and
Maharashtra languages in our vicinity are in an excellent state. The reason for this is not so much the poems of learned poets, but rather the eloquent series of sayings by educated modern individuals. In such a series of sayings, it is an essential duty to write about endless histories, moral, religious, and social matters, the conditions of time and place, the differences between ancient and modern, and the influence of health, fortune, knowledge, intellect, and humility, in a way that makes people understand and guides them to the right path. With this noble objective, monthly, weekly, and daily newspapers have emerged here and there, demonstrating the efficiency of journalists who tirelessly strive and venture,'
Shastri wrote in the first issue.

Referring to the plight of Kannada, he explained, 'When we see many Kannada newspapers emerging and struggling without proper sustenance (without subscribers), it makes us wonder how long this unfortunate situation of Kannadigas, who are Indians, will last. Newspapers of foreign countries, within a few days of their appearance, grow and continue to grow like the crescent moon because they find many patrons and encouragers. Gradually, these newspapers, with their eloquence, are astonishing the people of the world and becoming like textbooks. While newspapers in other languages are prospering, if Kannada newspapers are falling into such a deplorable state, it reveals the negative qualities of Kannadigas as a whole, such as laziness, indifference, and ignorance. What can be said about the Kannadigas of the Nizam region, who are extremely backward? The Kannada land in this region can be said to be just the two districts of Raichur and Gulbarga; even in these two districts, there is not as much interest in Kannada newspapers as there is in reading newspapers of other languages. It would not be untrue to say that educated people who are curious to publish newspapers in their own language are very rare. In such a situation, where should encouragers come from? All that has been said so far is a natural matter, yet for some years, we have had the desire to serve the Kannada language mother.'

In this issue, 'Dharmachintaratna' by Masigi M. Basavappa Shastri, 'Adhyatma Vichara' by K. Veerappa of Gulbarga, 'Anubhava Sutra' by Nayakal M.J. Basavaraja Shastri, and 'Subhashi Sudhazari' by Kandagallu Pandita Parvata Shastri were published. 'Dharma Sanjeevini', which ran for approximately ten years,
ceased publication due to lack of reader interest and financial difficulties.

To overcome financial difficulties, the Shankarananda Press printed appeal letters for fairs, festivals, and urs, handbills for plays and dramas, wedding invitations, advertisements for puranas and kirtanas, along with shop receipt books, account books, and admission and attendance registers for schools and colleges.

Later, the 'Shankarananda Press' was continued by his elder brother's son, Gurusiddha Shastri. Gurusiddha Shastri, himself a poet and writer, was also proficient in puranas and discourses alongside his writing. His 'Banadeshwara Suprabhata' of Kalebelagundi was popular. He wrote 'Charitre of Charabasavayya Tatana of Shahapur', 'Charitre of Nandur Malaya Mahanteshwara', 'Charitre of Gundappa Javalagi of Shivanagunda in Jevargi Taluk', 'Charitre of Vishwaradhyaru of Tumkur', Veerabhadra Vijaya (Musical Play),
and Mayakolahala (Musical Play). ('J.M. Gurusiddha Shastri' by A.K. Rameshwar in the series 'Kannada Kattidavaru').

Initially a teacher, Gurusiddha Shastri later left his teaching profession and fully dedicated himself to the printing press. Gurusiddha Shastri, who himself composed by arranging types, also did printing and binding.

B.M. Veereshwara Shastri, a teacher at the 'Shankara Sanskrit Pathashala' in Yadgir, started the 'Amaravani' (1949) newspaper. 'Amaravani' was also printed at the Shankarananda Press. Gurusiddha Shastri was the co-editor of 'Amaravani'. This newspaper gave priority to Vachana literature, similar to Halakatti's 'Sharana Sahitya'. In 1951, when the Shankara Sanskrit Vidyalaya was on the verge of closing, Veeresha Shastri, who was a teacher there, decided to go to Raichur. Along with him, the 'Amaravani' newspaper also shifted to Raichur.

Disheartened by this, Shastri intended to start a new newspaper himself. This led to the launch of the weekly newspaper 'Hyderabad Kannadiga'. In 'Hyderabad Kannadiga', which started in 1953, the poem 'Hottito Hottitu Kannadada Deepa, Arito Arito Shatamanagala Shapa' by poet Siddhayya Puranika was published. This newspaper was non-partisan and non-religious. However, it worked in favor of the linguistic province. In 1955, 'Hyderabad Kannadiga' sharply criticized the decision to divide Bidar district into three parts and distribute them among Mysore, Hyderabad, and Mumbai provinces. Along with articles supporting unification, this newspaper also published articles on the eradication of untouchability, prohibition, illiteracy, ignorance, and superstitions. Shastri had hoped that the number of readers would increase after unification. But that did not happen. Its publication had to be stopped in January 1957.

A few days later (1966), Vishwanathareddy Mudnal, a leader of the Lokaseva Sangha in Yadgir and a political figure, intended to start the 'Vishwakalyana' newspaper. Mudnal brought B. Mahadevappa, who was working at 'Prajavani' at the time, and started 'Vishwakalyana' on August 15, 1966. This newspaper was also
printed at the Shankarananda Press.

After a year or two, due to increased workload, Shastri hesitated to print the newspaper. Then Mudnal and Mahadevappa started 'Basava Mudranalaya' and continued printing the newspaper there. The famous Panchanga of Surapur was also printed here. The printing of 'Surapur Bhuvaneshwari Panchanga', prepared by Hebbal Sheshashastri, who had extensive experience in astrology, contributed to the popularity of the press. 'Shankarananda Press' was also at the forefront of printing Kannada books. 'Charabasaveshwara Purana', 'Vishwaradhyara Purana', 'Nalvara Kori Siddheshwara Purana' written by Jyotipur Chandrashekhara Shastri, and 'Sharanabasaveshwara Purana' written by Chennabasava Kavi, and 'Prabhulingeshwara Purana' written by Rastapur Bhimakavi found their publication in this press.

Gowdappa Sadhu of Afzalpur had a special love for the philosophical verses of Kadakol Madiwalappa. He wished to collect, print, and distribute them to the people. However, he lacked the necessary financial resources. So, Gowdappa came to Shankarananda Press and expressed his helplessness. Then, Gurusiddha Shastri agreed to print Kadakol Madiwalappa's philosophical verses on the condition that the payment would be made later. Thus, 'Kaivalya Vakyamruta' and 'Nija Leelamruta' of Madiwalappa, compiled by Gowdappa, were published. Gowdappa distributed Madiwalappa's philosophical verses to every home. He gave the money earned from sales to the press. Similarly, the devotional songs of Koodaluru Basavalinga Sharana, and the philosophical verses and Swaravachanas of Rampur Bakkappa, and
B.A. Sanadi's poetry collection 'Himagiriya Mudiyalli' were printed. After Gurusiddha Shastri, his son Basavaraja Shastri continued the printing work. After him, Basavaraja Shastri's son, Shivraj Shastri, the fourth generation, is now overseeing the printing press.

Basavaraja Shastri, who set out to 'work for Kannada', started a newspaper for that purpose. He opened a printing press for the newspaper's printing. Thus, the press that started printed not one, but three or four newspapers. Along with that, hundreds of books, almanacs, and 'commercial' items like pamphlets, wedding invitations, and posters were also printed. Thus, the contribution of Basavaraja Shastri and Gurusiddha Shastri to Kannada literature and journalism through the 'Shankarananda Press', which is currently on the verge of a century, is unique. ‌
