‘Saroja's Story’ stirred the conscience

Reading ‘Saroja's Story’ in the September issue of Mayura made my heart heavy. It was a state where words couldn't even come out. It felt like unfortunate people like Saroja serve as a touchstone for the self-reflection of the wave of freedom.

I was an admirer of Besagarahalli Ramanna through the story ‘Gandhi’. My admiration for him increased even more through this story. In a time when stories of false sentimentality and shallow intellect are abundant, Ramanna's story also appeared as a text for new generation storytellers. This story is a balanced equation of the storyteller's intellect and conscience, a story that stirs the conscience of the readers.

Saroja's tragic hunger still exists unexpressed in society. We sometimes speak with insensitivity, having lost the mindset to recognize it. Saroja seems to challenge such words. Even if she ate a lump of mud without anyone forcing her, the guilt of creating the necessity for her to eat it belongs to all of us. No one has a direct role in Saroja's death. But is it possible to view her death as natural? Isn't every unnatural death a wound to the conscience of society?

Besagarahalli has narrated this story with extreme sympathy. It is noteworthy that no one is blamed in the story. It was surprising that such a unique story had not been published until now. May all of Besagarahalli Ramanna's unpublished stories reach the readers in collection form as soon as possible.

S. Manjunath, Bengaluru
